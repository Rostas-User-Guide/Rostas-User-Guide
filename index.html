Rostas Coordinator Guide

Fair, automatic, kind rostering for churches and volunteer organisations.

Watch First — 3 minutes
Introducing Rostas

What is Rostas?

Rostas is your organisation's volunteer roster assistant. It handles the time-consuming, people-sensitive work of building rosters, collecting availability, and finding replacements when someone can't make it — automatically, fairly, and kindly.

Once set up, Rostas runs most of the roster cycle on its own. Your job shifts from juggling spreadsheets and chasing people by text to simply reviewing what Rostas has prepared and pressing go.

The old wayThe Rostas way
2–3 hours of emails, calls, and chasingUnder 1 hour via a guided 6-step wizard
Flat yes/no availability guesswork3-state availability + 4-tier role preferences
Coordinator manually calls around when someone cancelsAutomated SMS replacement chain handles it automatically
More risk of volunteer burnout from over-rosteringFrequency caps and self-serve opt-outs built in

How Rostas Works — The Big Picture

Rostas works in a rhythm that repeats every few weeks. The six-step Roster Wizard guides you through each stage in order:

StepNameWhat happens
1Confirm Dates & RolesRostas refreshes your seasons calendar, overlays public and school holidays, and confirms your roles list is up to date.
2Role PrefsVolunteers (or the coordinator) set which roles the volunteer is willing to perform — Primary, Fillable, or Not for me. Set once, remembered for every future build - editable in Profile pages.
3AvailabilityRostas can email each volunteer asking which upcoming dates they're available for or this step can be skipped by the coordinator. Volunteers tap a link in emails or fill or use the code from the text to go to the edit their availability dates.
4Build RosterThe engine automatically fills every slot using a four-tier priority system. The build takes 2 or 3 minutes.
5Check RosterYou review what Rostas has built using the traffic light system — green filled, amber pending, red unfilled.  Coordinator can add replacements or make swaps at this point if required. 
6NotifyRostas emails every rostered volunteer their assignments. Automatic reminders follow closer to the date — no further action needed.

Key Concepts

Availability — Simple for Volunteers

Before each build, Rostas can email your volunteers asking which upcoming dates they're available for. Volunteers receive a personalised link and see a simple calendar of dates. Everything starts as Available — volunteers only change the dates they can't make.

✅ Available
The default. Volunteer is free and happy to serve on this date.
❓ Not sure
Volunteer might be available. Rostas may provisionally assign them.
❌ Can't make it
Volunteer is unavailable. They won't be assigned to this date.
💡 If a volunteer doesn't respond, Rostas can send an automatic SMS nudge after a configurable number of days. No manual chasing needed.

The Intelligent Matching Engine

The roster engine doesn't fill slots blindly. It uses a four-tier priority system that respects both when volunteers can serve and what they actually want to do:

TierAvailabilityRole matchPriority
1✅ AvailablePrimary role (Yes, I do this)Highest — assigned first
2✅ AvailableFillable role (Happy to fill in)Second choice
3❓ Not surePrimary roleThird — provisional
4❓ Not sureFillable roleLast resort

Within each tier, Rostas selects the volunteer who has been rostered least recently — keeping things fair automatically over time without any manual tracking.

The Roster Board

The Board is your day-to-day view of the current roster. It shows every slot, every date, and every volunteer assignment at a glance. A traffic light colour system tells you instantly what's filled, what's pending, and what needs attention.

🟢 Filled
Volunteer confirmed. No action needed.
🟡 Pending
Provisionally assigned (volunteer marked Not sure). Monitor for confirmation.
🔴 Unfilled
No suitable volunteer found automatically. Needs your attention.

The Automated SMS Replacement Chain

When a rostered volunteer can't make it, they tap a link in their notification email. Rostas immediately starts working through the backup list — no coordinator involvement needed.

🌙 Quiet hours are always respected. Rostas never sends automated SMS during your quiet hours. If the replacement chain reaches a volunteer during that window, it pauses and resumes automatically once quiet hours end. You set the start and end times in Settings → SMS (the default is 9pm–8am).

When Rostas needs a reply by text — a replacement request, a manual-roster heads-up, or a cross-roster ask — the message contains a 4-digit code, not a link. New Zealand mobile networks silently filter many texts that contain web links, so a tappable link in an SMS can't be relied on to arrive. On SMS a short code does double duty: it survives carrier filtering and it's far shorter than a link, keeping each message inside the single-text limit so your church isn't charged for two.

The volunteer opens https://rostas.app/enter and types the 4-digit code from their text. The code is single-use and expires after a short window, so it can't be reused or forwarded. Once entered, they land on the same accept / decline page they'd have reached from a link.

📧 Email is unchanged. Email notifications aren't filtered and aren't charged per character, so they still contain ordinary tap-able links — only SMS moved to codes in NZ. Because the email carries the link and explains the entry page, it's how most volunteers first learn where rostas.app/enter is. They can bookmark it, and your church can link to it from your website behind the Rostas logo so it's always one tap away.

Looking After Your Volunteers

Rostas is designed with your volunteers in mind, not just your roster.

FeatureWhat it does for your volunteers
Frequency capsSet a maximum number of times each volunteer can be rostered per number of weeks. The engine never exceeds this, even if they're always available.
Quiet hoursNo automated SMS during the quiet window you set in Settings (default 9pm–8am). Volunteers are never disturbed at night.
Self-serve opt-outVolunteers can pause or opt out of SMS and email at any time using a link in any Rostas message — no need to contact you.  They may be happy just to print out their roster. 
Relational rosteringRostas tries to schedule family members and preferred buddies on the same dates — making it easier to serve together or share transport. 

Who Does What

Rostas is designed so that most of the routine work happens automatically. Here's how responsibilities divide between you, your volunteers, and Rostas:

WhoWhat they do
You — the coordinatorRun the 6-step wizard each cycle, review the built roster, handle exceptions, manage volunteers and settings
Your volunteersRespond to availability emails, set role preferences, accept or decline replacement requests by entering a short code from sms or tapping a link in an email. 
RostasEverything in between — building the roster, sending notifications, chasing replacements, sending reminders, enforcing frequency caps
✨ Rostas doesn't replace your judgement. There will be times when you want to override what Rostas has built — swap two people, add someone manually, or put a slot on hold. The Board makes all of this easy. Rostas handles the routine so you can focus on the exceptions.

Part B Setting Up Rostas First-run setup wizard · Adding volunteers · Your first roster type

When you open Rostas for the first time, the setup wizard runs automatically. It takes about five minutes and covers the essentials — your organisation name, your contact details, and a quick SMS test to make sure everything is working before your first roster build.

The wizard runs once. After it's complete, Rostas opens directly to the Builds dashboard on every future visit.

ℹ️ Before you start: Your Rostas coordinator needs to have set up the system and given you the link to open it. If you don't have a link yet, ask them for it.

The Setup Wizard

The wizard has four steps shown across the top of the screen. You can see at a glance how far through you are and what's coming next.

Step 1 — Your Organisation

The first step establishes your organisation's identity. The name you enter here appears in every message sent to volunteers — availability emails, roster notifications, and SMS reminders.

FieldWhat to enterRequired?
Organisation nameYour organisation's full name as you want it to appear to volunteers — e.g. "Riverside Community Church". Maximum 40 characters.Yes
TimezoneSelect your timezone from the dropdown. This controls all scheduling and quiet hours.Yes
Country / calling codeSelect your country. This is used to format volunteer phone numbers correctly for SMS delivery.Yes
WebsiteOptional. Your organisation's website URL — must start with https://No

Click Next → when all required fields are filled. The button stays disabled until everything is valid.

Step 2 — Your Details

Step 2 captures the primary coordinator's contact details. These are used for SMS test sends, coordinator escalation alerts when the replacement chain is exhausted, and the bottom of the coordinator app sidebar.

FieldWhat to enter
Your nameYour full name as coordinator — e.g. "Sarah Mitchell"
Your emailYour email address — receives error alerts and escalation notifications
Your mobileYour mobile number in full international format — e.g. +6421123456. Must include country code.

Step 3 — SMS Test

Before your first roster build, Rostas verifies that SMS is working. This step sends a real test message to your mobile so you can confirm everything is set up correctly.

How to complete Step 3

Your mobile number is pre-filled from Step 2. Tap Send test SMS → and wait for the message to arrive on your phone — usually within 30 seconds. Once received, a green confirmation appears and the Next button enables.

💡 If the test message doesn't arrive within a minute, check that your mobile number. You can edit the number in the field and try again. If it still fails, email support@rostas.app — SMS sending is set up by Rostas, not something you configure yourself.
ℹ️ If SMS isn't set up on your copy yet, Step 3 shows a "SMS isn't set up yet" screen instead of the test send, with a link to support@rostas.app and a Continue without SMS button. You can finish setup and start using Rostas by email — SMS replacement requests and reminders begin working once SMS is switched on.  Rostas uses Prepay SMS packs which you will soon be able to purchase.

Step 4 — Done

Once SMS is confirmed, the wizard completes and Rostas is ready to use.

From here you have two options:

  • Go to Build → — takes you straight to the Builds dashboard, ready to start your first roster cycle. This is the right choice if your volunteers are already imported.
  • Explore Settings — opens the Settings view if you want to review or customise anything before you import volunteers or do your first first build.

What's Next

Rostas is set up. Next, get your volunteers in: head to Part C — Profiles to import or add them and set up how each one serves. Once your profiles are in, Part D — The Roster Cycle walks you through building your first roster.

Part C Profiles Volunteers list · Adding · Editing · Volunteer detail

The Profiles section is where you manage everyone who serves in your organisation. Access it by clicking Profiles in the sidebar. From here you can view all volunteers, add new ones, edit details, view role lists, and manage per-roster settings like role preferences, frequency caps, and availability.

Importing your volunteers from CSV

The quickest way to get your volunteers into Rostas is to import a spreadsheet — from a previous roster system, a church database, or even a simple contact list. This is how most organisations start. If your volunteers already live in another system, you may be able to sync them automatically instead — see the Connector Appendix.

Open the Profiles list and click Import Volunteer CSV on the toolbar. The import runs in three steps.

💡 Your CSV needs at minimum a name and a mobile number. Email is strongly recommended so volunteers can receive availability requests and notifications. If your spreadsheet has a column for roster type, map it during the import and volunteers are assigned to the right rosters automatically. Phone numbers missing a country code are corrected for you.

Adding a volunteer manually

For one-offs — a single new volunteer between imports — click Add Volunteer on the Profiles toolbar. Fill in their details and save; they appear in the list immediately and can be included in your next build.

FieldWhat to enterRequired?
Full nameAs you want it to appear in rosters and messagesYes
Mobile e.g. +6421123456Yes (for SMS)
EmailFor notifications and availability requestsYes (for email)
Roster typesWhich rosters they serve in — tick all that applyYes

The profiles list

Once your volunteers are in, the Profiles list shows everyone — name, roster types, phone, and email — in one place. Search by name, email, or phone, or filter by Roster Type at the top of the page, or Active, Inactive, or All using the chips below the search bar. Click any row to open that volunteer's profile.

💡 Click any row to open that volunteer's full profile — where you edit their details, set the roles they do, their frequency, who they serve with, and view their availability.

The Profile Detail Page

Click any volunteer in the list to open their profile. This is where you manage everything about that person — their contact details, which rosters they serve in, the roles they do, how often they serve, who they serve alongside, and their availability. The left panel holds their contact details and a tab pill per roster type; the right panel holds the settings for whichever roster tab is selected, so each roster a volunteer serves in has its own independent setup.

Contact details & toggles

The left panel shows the volunteer's name, phone, email, and status, with four toggles that control how Rostas treats them:

ToggleWhat it controls
ActiveWhether this volunteer appears in roster builds. Turn off to pause someone without deleting them.
EmailWhether they receive email notifications. Turn off if they've opted out or prefer SMS only.
SMSWhether they receive SMS messages at all. Turn off if they've opted out of texts.
Always text Opts this volunteer in to the day-before reminder text even when the reminder mode would otherwise skip them (see Settings — reminder mode). Useful for volunteers who reliably miss email.  In settings Text non-responders (recommended) texts only volunteers with no email, those who haven't confirmed the reminder email, and anyone set to "Always text" in this profile details page. 

Roles — what they do

The Roles section on the right panel is where you set which roles this volunteer performs in this roster. Each role shows as Does This, Fills In, or Doesn't Do 

Linking roles together

Sometimes two roles should travel together — Say a worship leader always also  plays an acoustic guitar.   Role linking handles this. Both roles must be set to Does This first; then click the link icon (🔗) on a role card to open a dropdown of roles you can link it to, and choose the link type.

Link typeHow it behaves
Dominant — ShadowOne-way pairing. When the dominant role is filled by this volunteer, Rostas pulls them into the shadow role on the same date too — but not the other way around. Use it when one role should drag a second along with it.  For example when Sam is worship leading he always plays acoustic guitar, but when Sam is rostered to play acoustic guitar he is not always worship leading. 
Equal PairingTwo-way pairing . The two roles are always scheduled together — the volunteer is placed in both or neither. Use it when a volunteer always wants to do both roles together.  

Linked roles are ringed on the profile so you can see the relationship at a glance.

A gold drop-shadow marks the dominant role, a grey drop-shadow its shadow pairing. 

A highlighted green circle boarder marks paired roles in profile settings. 


On the Board, the matching assignment cards carry the same colours.

💡 To change or remove a link, click the link icon on an already-linked role — the dropdown then shows the linked partner and an option to unlink.

Frequency — how often they serve

The Frequency control — labelled ONCE EVERY — caps how often this volunteer is rostered in this roster, so nobody is over-used even when they're always available. Tap a pill: No cap, or once every 1wk through 12wk.

ℹ️ The cap counts distinct service dates, not roles. A volunteer rostered to two roles on the same day counts as one service — so serving twice in one Sunday doesn't use up two of their allocation.

Relational rostering — serving together

Relational rostering asks Rostas to place people together on the same dates — a Buddy they like to serve alongside, or a Household so family members share the same rota. Turn on "Serves With" and pick who.

ℹ️ Relational rostering is not the same as role linking. Role linking ties two roles together for the same person; relational rostering places two people on the same dates. One is about jobs, the other about company.

Next roster availability

The availability grid shows this volunteer's responses for the upcoming dates — the same three states they set on their own form:

🟢 Available
Free to serve on this date.
🟡 Unsure
Might be available — Rostas may provisionally assign them.
🔴 Not available
Can't make it — won't be assigned.
⚠️ If the season's dates aren't confirmed yet, an yellow banner appears at the bottom of this section — It is good to get season dates confirmed . Adding or deleting dates helps volunteers accurately know dates for the next roster.
💡 Searching across rosters. The prev/next arrows at the top of the profile step through volunteers. Next to them is a search-scope toggle (people icons) — switch it on to search across every roster rather than just the current one.

Adding a Volunteer to a New Roster

If a volunteer starts serving in an additional roster type, click the inactive roster tab on their detail page. A confirmation dialog appears — confirm to add them to that roster. Their settings for the new roster start fresh and can be configured from the right panel.

Part D The Roster Cycle New Roster Wizard · Roster Wizard · The Build Menu

There are two roster wizards in Rostas. The New Roster Wizard runs once when you set up a new roster type — it defines the name, roles, season dates, and volunteers. The Roster Wizard runs every few weeks to collect availability, build the roster, and send notifications to volunteers.

Both start from the Builds dashboard.

The Builds Dashboard

The Builds dashboard is your home base for all roster activity. Open it by clicking Builds in the sidebar. Every roster type appears as a full-width row — one per roster — so you can see the state of your whole operation at a glance and act on any roster without leaving the page.

The state button

The button on the left of each row is dynamic — its colour and label tell you exactly where that roster is in its cycle, and clicking it always picks up at the right next step. You never have to remember where you left off.


StateWhat it meansClicking it…
First SetupA brand-new roster type that hasn't been built yet.Starts the first build.
Season ConfirmedThe season's dates are set and it's ready to begin a build.Starts building.
GatheringAvailability emails have gone out and responses are coming in — the state you'll sit in for a few days each cycle. (You'll also see it briefly while the roster itself builds.)Continues where you left off.
Ready to SendBuilt and reviewed — waiting for you to send notifications.Continues to the notify step.
ActiveNotifications sent — the roster is live for the current cycle.Starts the next rebuild.
PausedThe roster is paused — no builds, reminders, or replacements run.Resumes the roster.

The date-chip strip

Each row shows a strip of small date chips — the roster's recent and upcoming service dates. The colours are deliberately simple: blue means a date that has already passed, and green means an upcoming date. It's an at-a-glance sense of where each roster sits in time.

ℹ️ These two colours aren't the same as the date bands in the wizard. On the Builds dashboard, chip colour just means past (blue) or upcoming (green). Inside the Confirm Dates step of a build you'll see a richer set of coloured bands for last build, this build, next build, and excluded dates — that's a different view with a different job.

On the right of each row, the ⋯ menu holds the rest of what you can do with a roster — pausing, adjusting dates and roles, resetting, or deleting. See The Build Menu below for the full walkthrough.

💡 Starting a brand-new roster type? A New Roster option on the Builds dashboard sets one up from scratch. See the New Roster Wizard section below.

The New Roster Wizard

Run this wizard when you're setting up a roster type for the first time — for example, adding a Music roster or a Hospitality team. It takes about 10 minutes and walks you through five steps: Name & Icon, Roles, Season, Volunteers, and Review.

ℹ️ You only run the New Roster Wizard once per roster type. After setup is complete, all future roster builds use the Roster Wizard instead.

Step 1 — Name & Icon

Give your roster a short name and choose an icon. The name appears in SMS messages, emails, and the coordinator app — so keep it brief and clear.

Step 2 — Roles

Define the roles that need to be filled each time this roster runs. For each role, set a minimum and maximum number of people required. You can add, rename, or retire roles later from Managing Roles.

💡 Role names matter. Use clear, specific names that volunteers will recognise — e.g. "Sound Desk", "Welcome Team", "Data Projection". These names appear in SMS messages and emails sent to volunteers.

Step 3 — Season

A season defines the date range, frequency, and day of week for this roster. Rostas uses seasons to calculate which dates to roster for, and to overlay public and school holidays automatically.

FieldWhat to enter
Start dateThe first date this roster will run
End dateThe last date of a fixed-term season (a perpetual roster needs no end date — see Season type below)
FrequencyDaily, Weekly, Fortnightly, 1st and 3rd, 2nd and 4th, or Monthly
Day of weekWhich day the roster runs — e.g. Sunday, Monday
Season typeFixed term (has a real end date) or Perpetual (ongoing — auto-extends). See Season type for the difference.
Pause school holidaysTick if this roster doesn't run during school holidays
Pause public holidaysTick if this roster doesn't run on public holidays

Step 4 — Volunteers

Select which volunteers from your list will serve on this roster. Tick each volunteer you want to include. Only volunteers already in Rostas appear here — if someone is missing, add them in Profiles first then return to this step.

Step 5 — Review

The final step shows a summary of everything you've set up — roster name, icon, roles, season dates, and volunteer count. Review it carefully before confirming.

Click Confirm & Create Roster to finish. Your new roster type appears on the Builds dashboard immediately. You're now ready to run your first Roster Wizard for this roster.

The Roster Wizard

The Roster Wizard is the recurring roster cycle you run every few weeks. Each roster type has its own — the page is headed "[Your roster] Roster Wizard" — and it takes you through the same six named steps every time: Confirm Dates & Roles, Role Prefs, Availability, Build Roster, Check Roster, and Notify. A progress rail across the top shows where you are, marking each step Completed, Skipped, In progress, or still to come.

To start, open the Builds dashboard and click a roster's state button. If a build is already in progress, you're taken straight back to the step you left off at.

Step 1 — Confirm Dates & Roles

This first step is where you confirm two things for the build: which dates it covers, and which roles (and how many people per role) are in play. Behind the scenes it also refreshes your seasons calendar and overlays public and school holidays, so the dates are correct before availability goes out.

ℹ️ "Refresh & Sync" is an action within this step, not a step of its own. You may see a Refresh & Sync button here — it re-pulls the calendar and holidays. It's part of confirming your dates, which is why the step is named Confirm Dates & Roles.

Step 2 — Role Prefs

This step lets volunteers update their role preferences — which roles they're willing to perform. You can send a role-preference email to all volunteers, or skip the step entirely.

💡 This step is commonly skipped. Many coordinators set each volunteer's roles directly on the Profile page ahead of time, which makes this step unnecessary for that build — the rail simply marks it "Skipped" and you move on. Send the emails at the start of a new season or when new volunteers join; skip it for a routine  rebuild.

Step 3 — Availability

Rostas emails all active volunteers asking which upcoming dates they're available. Each volunteer receives a personalised link — no login required. They tap their dates, mark any they can't make, and save.

⏰ Allow 5–7 days for responses before moving to Step 4. Volunteers who don't respond within a configurable number of days automatically receive an SMS nudge.

Step 4 — Build Roster

The roster engine analyses every volunteer — their availability, role preferences, how recently they've served, and their frequency cap — and fills every slot automatically. The build takes a few minutes for most rosters.

⚙️ The engine uses a four-tier priority system — see Part A, The Intelligent Matching Engine for full detail on how slots are filled.

Step 5 — Check Roster

Before anything is sent to volunteers, you review what Rostas has built. Clicking Open Roster Board opens the board in review mode — you can see every assignment, make any manual changes, and return to the wizard. The Communications Preview in Settings lets you see exactly what each volunteer will receive before you send.

🟢 Filled
Volunteer confirmed. Notification ready to send.
🟡 Pending
Provisionally assigned (volunteer marked Not sure). Will receive a confirm/decline message.
🔴 Unfilled
No volunteer found. Address before sending if possible.
⚠️ Don't skip this step. It's your last chance to catch problems before volunteers are notified. Check for unfilled slots, confirm the message wording looks right, and make any manual swaps needed.

Step 6 — Notify

Once you're satisfied with the roster, click Send Notifications Now. Every rostered volunteer receives a personalised email with their assignments and a "Can't make it?" link.

📬 What happens automatically after Step 6:
  • Email reminder sent 3 days before each service date
  • Depending on Settings SMS reminder can be sent 1 day before each service date
  • If a volunteer taps "Can't make it" — the SMS replacement chain starts automatically
No further action needed until the next roster cycle.

The Build Menu

The ⋯ menu on the right of each row on the Builds dashboard holds everything you do with a roster between builds — pausing it, adjusting its dates or roles, changing how often it runs, clearing its data, or deleting it outright. Click it to open a pop-up of options.

OptionWhat it does
Pause roster / Resume rosterTemporarily stops all activity for this roster — no builds, reminders, or replacements — without losing any history. The same option resumes it. See below.
Manage DatesOpens the Build Dates screen on its own, so you can adjust which dates this roster runs on — remove one, shift the build boundary, or add a one-off extra date. See Confirming Build Dates below. Changes apply from the next build onward — dates already built are never changed retroactively.
Manage rolesOpens the roles screen for this roster — add, rename, set headcounts, or deactivate roles. See Managing Roles below. Also applies from the next build onward.
FrequencyOpens the Frequency page, where you set how often the roster runs and its season type. See Frequency & Season Type below.
Reset roster dataWipes this roster type's operational data — assignments, availability, replacement history — while keeping the roster shell, its roles, your volunteers, and your settings intact. See Resetting Roster Data below.
Delete rosterPermanently removes the roster type and everything tied to it. See Deleting a Roster below.

Pause / Resume roster

Pausing a roster stops all activity for it immediately — no builds, reminders, or replacement chains run — without losing any history. The menu option and the row's state button both switch to Resume roster while paused; click either to pick back up exactly where you left off.

Confirming Build Dates

Before each build you confirm exactly which dates it covers, on the Build Dates screen. It lays out your roster's dates in three bands — what's already built, what this build will cover, and what's coming next — and lets you drop a date you don't want, shift the boundary between builds, or add a one-off extra date such as Christmas Day or Good Friday.

You reach it two ways: as the first step of a build (Confirm Dates & Roles), or on its own from the Builds dashboard — ⋯ — Manage Dates.

BandWhat it means
Last BuildDates already built in the previous cycle. Each card shows a padlock — these are locked and can't be changed here. If something needs fixing on a date that's already built, do it on the Board instead.
This BuildThe dates the next build will cover — the band header shows a running count (e.g. "This Build · 9 dates"). These are being confirmed right now; clicking Build will roster them.
Next BuildDates coming after this build, also with a running count. Volunteers can already set their availability for these — it's a look-ahead so nothing catches them by surprise.
ExcludedA date you've removed from This Build or Next Build — turned pink so it won't be rostered.

Removing a date

Click the × on any card in This Build or Next Build to exclude it — the card turns pink and drops out of the build. Click it again to bring the date back. Cards in Last Build can't be removed this way, since they're already built.

Shifting the boundary between builds

A small chevron sits on the date at the boundary between This Build and Next Build. Click it to move that date across the line — pulling the next upcoming date into This Build to extend how far this build reaches, or sending the last date of This Build back into Next Build to shorten it.

Adding an extra date

Use the date field and label box beneath This Build to add a one-off service outside the normal pattern — Christmas Eve, Good Friday, a special event. Pick the date, type a short label (e.g. "Good Friday"), and click + Add Date. The new card appears in This Build with your label shown as a tag, and is included the next time you build.

ℹ️ Holidays are tagged automatically — but tagging and pausing aren't the same thing. Dates that fall directly on a public holiday or a school holiday are labelled for you, and look distinct from dates you've excluded by hand, so you can tell an automatic holiday from a deliberate choice. Long weekends (Labour Weekend, Anzac Weekend, and others) get their own distinct yellow tag too — but the tag alone doesn't pause anything. Only a direct hit on the public holiday or school holiday itself pauses the roster (and only if you've ticked Pause public holidays / Pause school holidays for this roster type). A long-weekend Sunday that isn't itself the public holiday still needs rostering as normal — exclude it by hand if you don't want it covered.
💡 Confirm early. Ideally do this a few weeks before the dates arrive, so there's time to collect availability and build. A perpetual roster rolls forward on its own, so each cycle you're simply confirming the next stretch of dates; a fixed-term roster is confirmed up to its end date. (See Frequency & Season Type for the difference.)
ℹ️ Nothing changes until you click Save. Removing, shifting, or adding dates only takes effect once you click Save at the bottom of the screen — leaving without saving discards your changes. When you open this screen standalone via Manage Dates, saved changes apply from the next build onward; dates already built are never altered.

Managing Roles

Once a roster exists, you can add, rename, re-size, or retire its roles at any time. Open the Builds dashboard, click the ⋯ menu on the roster's row, and choose Manage roles. The page lists every role for that roster with its headcount and an active switch.

ActionHow
Add a roleUse the add option at the bottom of the list; enter the name and its minimum and maximum headcount.
Rename a roleUse Rename on the role. The new name appears in future messages to volunteers.
Change headcountAdjust the minimum and maximum for how many people the role needs each time.
Deactivate a roleSwitch it to Inactive. It drops out of future builds but keeps its history, and you can reactivate it any time.
ℹ️ Changes apply from your next build onward. Renaming a role, changing its headcount, switching it active or inactive, or adding a new role all take effect at the next build — dates you've already built keep their original roles and wording. Nothing changes underneath the volunteers you've already notified.
💡 Deactivate rather than delete. Deactivating a role keeps the full history of who served in it; it just stops appearing in new builds. Reach for deactivate unless you're correcting a genuine setup mistake.
⚠️ Mind the timing when renaming mid-cycle. Because a rename only affects messages sent after it, volunteers already notified will have the old name in their earlier email or text. If you rename partway through a cycle, expect a brief mismatch until the next build.

Frequency & Season Type

There are two separate frequency ideas in Rostas: how often the roster runs, and how often each volunteer serves. This section covers the roster side — its cadence and whether the season is fixed or ongoing. The per-volunteer cap is set on each person's profile (see Frequency on the Profile detail page); the two are explained together at the end.

Roster cadence — how often the roster runs

Not every roster runs weekly. A community meal might run fortnightly, a kids' ministry only on the 1st and 3rd Sunday, a review team monthly. The Frequency page sets this cadence per roster type — it decides which dates appear in the season, and therefore which dates Rostas builds for. You reach it two ways: when you first create a roster (in the New Roster Wizard's first step), or any time afterwards from the Builds dashboard — ⋯ — Frequency.

OptionWhen to use it
DailyRoster runs every day it applies — e.g. a hospital-visit or meals roster
WeeklyRoster runs every week — e.g. Sunday service
FortnightlyRoster runs every two weeks
1st and 3rdRuns on the 1st and 3rd occurrence of the day each month
2nd and 4thRuns on the 2nd and 4th occurrence of the day each month
MonthlyRuns once a month — e.g. a quarterly review team
💡 Cadence is set once per roster type and applies to everyone in it. If two groups run on different weeks, set them up as separate roster types.

Season type — fixed-term or perpetual

Below the cadence pills, the Season Type control decides whether this roster has an end or simply keeps going:

TypeWhat it means
Fixed-termThe season has a real start and end date. Use it for anything with a natural finish — a school term, a set programme, a holiday series.
Perpetual (ongoing)The roster runs indefinitely with no end date to manage. Rostas keeps enough weeks generated ahead automatically, so a perpetual roster never runs out of dates.
ℹ️ Perpetual seasons roll forward on their own — the end date is maintained by Rostas, so you never have to extend them. Most ongoing church rosters (Sunday service, welcome team) suit Perpetual. If you later need a perpetual roster to stop on a set date, switch it to Fixed-term and give it an end date.

Volunteer frequency cap — how often a person serves

Separate from the roster's cadence, each volunteer has their own cap — how often they personally get rostered — so nobody is used every week just because they're always free. You set it on each person's profile (Frequency on the Profile detail page), independently for each roster they serve in. The two settings work together:

ExampleRoster cadenceVolunteer capResult
Sunday team volunteerWeeklyOnce every 2wkRostered roughly once a fortnight
Sound desk specialistWeeklyNo capRostered most Sundays (willing and able)
Occasional helperFortnightlyOnce every 4wkRostered roughly once a month
💡 The cap counts distinct service dates, not individual roles. A volunteer in two roles on the same date counts as one service — so serving twice on one Sunday doesn't use two of their allocation.

Resetting Roster Data

Reset roster data clears a roster type's operational history — built assignments, availability responses, replacement chains — without touching the roster's setup: its roles, volunteers, and season stay exactly as they are. It's a pick → preview → confirm flow: choose the roster type, review what will be cleared, then confirm.

💡 Built for getting started. This is the tool for wiping sample or test data before a roster goes live — clear out anything built while you were setting up, then start rostering for real without deleting and re-creating the whole roster type.
⚠️ Reset vs Delete. Reset clears history but keeps the roster type itself — its roles and volunteer assignments to it survive. Delete removes the roster type entirely — see Deleting a Roster below. If you're clearing out test data before going live, Reset is almost always what you want.

Deleting a Roster

Delete roster permanently removes the roster type — its shell, roles, seasons, build history, and every assignment tied to it. Unlike Reset, there's no going back.

⚠️ Deleting is permanent. If you only need to stop a roster for a while — over summer, say — use Pause from the ⋯ menu instead. Pausing keeps all the roster's history and settings; deleting removes them for good.

The Roster Board is where you manage the roster between builds. You'll come here to check on replacement progress, make manual swaps, confirm pending assignments, or search for a specific volunteer. Access it by clicking Boards in the sidebar.

Reading the Board

The Board shows every roster date as a row, with roles as columns. Each cell shows the assigned volunteer's name and date. Colour coding tells you the status of each assignment at a glance.

The legend row at the top of the board shows the five cell states. Swapping two volunteers

When two rostered volunteers want to trade dates, use Swap with… from the action menu. Click the first volunteer's cell, choose Swap with…, and that card is "armed" — it takes a gold border. Then click the cell you want to swap it with.

ℹ️ Conflict warnings are advisory, not blocking. If a swap would clash — say a volunteer is already rostered elsewhere that day — Rostas shows amber warnings on the confirmation, but you can still go ahead. It flags the risk and leaves the decision to you.

Manual Replacement

When you click Replace, the replacement panel appears below the action bar showing all available volunteers for that role and date, sorted by priority tier. Each volunteer shows their availability status and how recently they've been rostered.

💡 The tiers match the engine. Tier 1 volunteers are available and have this as a primary role — the ideal replacement. Lower tiers are less ideal but still valid options. Pick the best fit for the situation.
📬 The replacement volunteer is notified automatically. Once you click Select, Rostas sends them an SMS and email with their assignment details. You don't need to contact them separately.

Assigning a Second Role on the Same Date

Occasionally a volunteer may need to cover two roles on the same date — for example, stepping in for a missing team member while also serving in their usual role. Rostas supports this but flags it clearly so you're making a deliberate choice rather than an accidental double-booking.

⚠️ Use this sparingly. Volunteers serving in multiple roles in one service can feel stretched. Always confirm with the volunteer before assigning a second role.

The SMS Replacement Chain

When a volunteer taps "Can't make it" in their notification email — or when you click Save as declined on the board — Rostas starts the automated SMS replacement chain. It works through available volunteers one at a time until someone accepts.

What happensTiming
Rostas identifies the next eligible volunteer and sends them an SMSImmediately (within quiet hours)
If they accept — slot is filled, original volunteer releasedAs soon as they reply
If they decline or don't respond — next volunteer is contactedAfter 2 hours (normal) or 30 min (urgent, within 24hrs of service)
If no one accepts — coordinator receives an escalation SMSAfter list is exhausted

The request text carries a 4-digit code rather than a link — NZ networks filter many link-bearing texts, and a code is shorter and cheaper to send. The volunteer enters it at rostas.app/enter to reach the accept / decline page. See Part A for the full explanation of code entry.

🌙 Quiet hours apply to the SMS chain. Messages are never sent during your quiet hours (set in Settings → SMS, default 9pm–8am). If the chain reaches a volunteer inside that window it pauses automatically and resumes once quiet hours end. You can monitor progress on the board — cells in the SMS chain state show in blue.

Searching for a Volunteer

Use the search field at the top right of the board to find a specific volunteer. As you type, their assignments are highlighted across the entire board — making it easy to see all their upcoming dates at once. Use the ◀ ▶ arrows to step through volunteers alphabetically. The Frequency panel shows how often the current volunteer is rostered and when they last served.

💡 Use volunteer search before making a manual replacement. Search for the volunteer you're thinking of replacing them with first — check they're not already heavily rostered that period before adding another date.
Part F Settings & Troubleshooting Configuration · SMS templates · Common problems · System status

The Settings view is where you configure how Rostas behaves — your organisation's identity, SMS credentials, message wording, timing rules, and more. Open it by clicking Settings in the left sidebar.

The Settings View

Finding your way around

The Settings view has two parts that work together.

The chip strip runs across the top and lets you jump directly to any category — Org, Contacts, SMS, Wording, Timing, Controls, System Status, and Advanced. Clicking a chip scrolls the left panel to that section instantly. The Communications chip (gold, with an arrow) opens the Communications Preview in place of Settings — use it to see how your message templates look before sending.

The search bar (top right, "Search settings…") filters the left panel live as you type. It searches across setting names and descriptions simultaneously — so typing "SMS" shows not just the SMS category but any setting whose description mentions SMS, across all categories. Non-matching rows disappear; categories with no matches collapse. Clear the search to restore the full list.

The two-column layout

  • Left panel — the scrollable list of all settings, grouped by category. Each row shows the setting name, a one-line description, and its current value on the right. Click any row to open it for editing.
  • Right panel — appears when you click a row. Shows the full description, an example value, the input field, and Save/Cancel buttons. The panel stays fixed while you scroll the left panel, so you can read and compare other settings without losing your place.

To close the right panel without saving, click Cancel or click a different row.

Editing a setting

  1. Click the row in the left panel. The right panel opens showing the setting name, description, and current value pre-filled in the input.
  2. Make your change. The input type matches the setting — dropdowns for timezone and country code, toggles for on/off settings, text fields for everything else.
  3. Validation runs as you type or on blur. If the value is invalid (wrong format, missing required content) the field turns red with an explanation. The Save button stays disabled until the value is valid.
  4. Click Save. The left panel updates immediately to show the new value — no page reload needed.

Using System Status

The System Status card and the settings list below it are designed to work together. The card tells you what's wrong; the settings list is where you fix it.

Treat System Status as your starting point every time you open the Settings page. Before searching for a specific setting or making a planned change, glance at the Warnings column first. If it shows green — "All systems go" — you can proceed. If it shows amber or red, deal with those first, because some warnings mean volunteers aren't receiving messages right now.

Understanding your roster's rhythm

Once warnings are clear, the lower sections of the card give you a different kind of visibility — not problems to fix, but patterns to understand. Two of them are worth checking regularly.

  • Recent Activity — a running list of the last twenty things Rostas did, newest first, across every level (routine actions, warnings, and errors). This is your first-stop diagnostic: if a volunteer says they didn't get a message, or you're not sure whether a job ran, this list shows what actually happened and when. A steady stream of recent entries means Rostas is ticking over normally; a list whose newest entry is days old may mean the daily triggers have stopped running.
  • Communication Failures — the most direct troubleshooting tool, shown in the Warnings column. When all is well it reads "No comms failures"; otherwise it lists recent send errors and warnings. If a volunteer tells you they never received a replacement SMS, check here for an entry on the date in question. If you see nothing at all for that date, the message may have been suppressed before sending — check SMS enabled, test mode, and quiet hours.

The card also shows activity and communications counts over the past seven days, plus replacement outcomes once you've run a few cycles. A high replacement-timeout rate can mean your replacement timers are too short for how quickly your volunteers reply, or that quiet hours are limiting the available sending window.

💡 A coordinator who builds this habit — open Settings, check System Status, fix any warnings, then do what you came to do — will catch problems before volunteers notice them rather than after.

Settings Categories

  • Organisation — your organisation's name, tagline, timezone, country calling code, contact email, website, and volunteer-facing logo. The organisation name is the most important field: it appears in every SMS and email sent to volunteers as [OrgName]. Get this right first.
  • Coordinator Contacts — your name, email, and mobile, plus per-roster coordinator contacts for Sunday, Music, Nourish, and any other roster types you've added. The per-roster contacts receive escalation alerts when a replacement chain is exhausted with no one found.
  • SMS — the SMS kill switch, test mode toggle, quiet hours, and replacement timers. See SMS settings in detail below.
  • Message Wording — every SMS template and email text block that Rostas sends to volunteers. SMS templates show a live character counter and required placeholder checker. See Message wording below.
  • Timing & Behaviour — how many weeks ahead to build rosters, freeze weeks, availability form deadline, reminder timing, and public holiday region. The Public holidays region field includes a dropdown of supported countries and a "Fetch holidays now" button — use this to pull the latest public holidays for your country whenever you update this setting.
  • Quick Controls — per-roster active toggles and email test mode. Useful for temporarily hiding a roster from the Build wizard without deleting it, or for switching email test mode on and off quickly.
  • Advanced — settings that affect system internals (web app URL, roster type list, AWS SMS keys). These are locked by default. Click Unlock to edit in the right panel and read the warning carefully before changing anything here. Incorrect values can stop roster builds or break SMS links. Contact tech support if unsure.

SMS Settings in Detail

Three settings here are worth understanding before you go live.

⚠️

SMS enabled — the master kill switch. When this is false, no SMS leaves Rostas at all — not replacements, not reminders, not test sends. The System Status card shows a red warning when this is off. Only disable it deliberately (e.g. during a migration or if your Twilio account lapses).

⚠️

SMS test mode — when true, every SMS that would go to a volunteer is redirected to the coordinator mobile instead. The message is prefixed with [TEST - would go to candidate]. Use this while setting up or testing replacement chains. Turn it off before your first real roster cycle. The System Status card shows an amber warning when test mode is on.

Quiet hours — SMS quiet hours start and end are set as 24-hour values (e.g. 21 = 9pm, 8 = 8am). Rostas will not send any SMS outside this window, including replacement chain advances. If a replacement request hits quiet hours, it waits until the quiet period ends before texting the next candidate. The default is 9pm–8am.

Replacement timers — three escalation speeds:

  • Normal (default 120 min) — used when the service is more than 24 hours away
  • Urgent (default 30 min) — used when the service is within 24 hours
  • Panic (default 10 min) — used when the service is within 2 hours

If volunteers are frequently timing out before responding, consider increasing the normal timer. If you're often scrambling at short notice, reducing the urgent or panic timers gets through the candidate list faster.

Reminder SMS mode — controlling reminder texts

The day-before reminder text is the single biggest driver of SMS cost — one text per rostered volunteer, every service. The Reminder SMS mode setting decides who actually receives it. (The 3-day reminder email is unaffected and still goes to everyone with an email address.)

ModeWho gets the day-before text
Text everyoneEvery rostered volunteer gets a text, even if they already confirmed by email.
Email-first (recommended)Texts only those who need it: volunteers with no email address, those who haven't confirmed the reminder email, and anyone set to "Always text too". Keeps SMS costs down.
Text no-oneNo day-before texts at all. Overrides "Always text too".
💡 Email-first is the default, and the best value for most churches. It leans on free email and only spends a text where it'll actually help — someone with no email, or who hasn't confirmed. For the few volunteers who reliably miss email, turn on "Always text too" on their profile so they still get a text under Email-first.

Message Wording

Every SMS template has three protections built in to prevent accidental breakage.

  • Character counter — SMS messages over 160 characters are split into two by carriers and charged as two texts. The counter below the textarea turns amber at 140 characters and red at 161+. The Save button is disabled when the count is red. The SMS templates are deliberately terse for this reason — when you edit one, the counter is protecting your church's SMS costs, not just formatting.
  • Required placeholders — each template has a set of placeholders that must be present for it to work correctly. For example, the replacement request SMS must include [Code] — without it, the volunteer has no code to enter. (The equivalent email templates require [Link] instead.) Missing placeholders are shown in red below the textarea. Save is blocked until all required placeholders are present.
  • Preview SMS — click this button to see the template rendered with realistic sample values substituted in ([Name] → Sarah, [Role] → Welcome Team, [Date] → today's date, [Code] → a sample 4-digit code such as 4821). This shows you exactly what a volunteer will read, at the correct character count. It does not send a real SMS.
ℹ️ All placeholder names use square brackets: [Name], [Role], [Date], [Link], [Code], [OrgName], [Deadline]. Never use curly braces — {name} will not be substituted.

The Message Wording category holds every message Rostas sends — the SMS templates (availability nudge, day-before reminder, replacement request, replacement confirmed, coordinator escalation) and the email templates (roster notification, availability request, reminder, swap). Edit any of them here; the three protections above apply to each.

⚠️ Two templates here are editable but not currently sent. The Original volunteer released SMS and the Decline confirmation SMS appear in the list and preview, but Rostas does not currently text the original volunteer when a replacement is found, nor the volunteer who declines. A volunteer who taps "Can't make it" sees a web page — "No problem — we'll find someone else" — rather than a text. You can edit their wording so it's ready, but don't rely on either going out yet.

Template Placeholders

SMS and email templates use placeholders in square brackets that Rostas replaces with real values when sending. Never remove a required placeholder — if a required one is missing, the Save button is blocked.

PlaceholderWhat it becomes

[Date]The date of the service

Troubleshooting

Most issues in Rostas fall into a small number of categories. Work through this list before contacting tech support.

Volunteers aren't receiving SMS messages

  • Check Settings → Quick Controls → SMS_ENABLED is True
  • Check SMS_TEST_MODE is False — if True, all messages go to the coordinator only
  • Check the volunteer's SMS toggle is On in their detail page
  • Check quiet hours — SMS won't send between 9pm and 8am
  • Check the volunteer's mobile number is in correct international format (+64...)
  • Check the System Status communication failures table for error messages on the relevant date

Volunteers aren't receiving emails

  • Check EMAIL_TEST_MODE is False in Quick Controls
  • Check the volunteer's Email toggle is On in their detail page
  • Ask the volunteer to check their spam/junk folder
  • Confirm their email address is correct in their volunteer detail page

Roster has unfilled slots after building

  • Check that enough volunteers have responded to the availability email
  • Check that volunteers have role preferences set — without preferences set, the engine can't assign them to specific roles
  • Check frequency caps — volunteers who've hit their monthly cap won't be assigned further
  • Consider rebuilding after more availability responses come in
  • Use manual replacement on the Board to fill remaining slots

SMS replacement chain isn't progressing

  • Check quiet hours — chain pauses between 9pm and 8am and resumes automatically
  • Check SMS_ENABLED is True
  • Check there are eligible volunteers left on the backup list — if the list is exhausted you'll receive an escalation SMS
  • Use manual replacement on the Board to assign someone directly

When to contact tech support

Contact your tech support person if:

  • SMS messages are failing with error messages in the System Status communication failures table
  • The web app URL has changed and volunteer links are broken
  • You need to change Twilio or other API credentials
  • Something is producing repeated errors that don't resolve on their own
  • You need to add or remove a roster type
  • Anything requiring access to the Google Apps Script editor
📞 When contacting tech support, include a description of what you were doing, what you expected to happen, and what actually happened. A screenshot of any error message or the System Status panel is very helpful.
Appendix Connectors Syncing volunteers from an existing system

Most organisations get their volunteers into Rostas by importing a CSV — it works for everyone and needs nothing more than a spreadsheet. But if your church already keeps its people in another system, a connector can sync them into Rostas automatically, so you don't maintain two lists by hand.

ℹ️ Connectors sync identity only. A connector brings across each person's name, mobile, and email — nothing more. Everything about how they serve — which rosters they're in, the roles they do, their frequency, who they serve with — is set up and lives entirely inside Rostas. Your other system stays the source of truth for contact details; Rostas stays the source of truth for rostering.

Infoodle

Infoodle is the first connector available in Rostas. Once it's connected, new and updated contacts flow in on their own — a volunteer added in Infoodle appears in your Profiles list without a re-import, and a changed phone number or email updates itself.

When someone is removed in Infoodle, Rostas doesn't delete them out from under you. Instead it flags them with a "Deleted in Infoodle" marker in the Profiles list, so you can confirm they should go and remove them here deliberately — protecting you from losing a volunteer's history by accident.

🔌 Setting up the Infoodle connector is done with Rostas, not self-serve. The connection uses credentials that are provisioned for your church rather than entered by a coordinator — so if you'd like Infoodle sync switched on, contact support@rostas.app and it'll be set up for you.

Other connectors

Infoodle is the first of a growing family of connectors. Support for other church and volunteer platforms — such as Planning Center and Elvanto — is planned, so more organisations can sync rather than import over time.

ℹ️ Not on the list yet? These connectors are on the roadmap rather than available today, and CSV import works with every system in the meantime — most spreadsheets and databases can export a CSV. If a connector for your platform would help, let your Rostas contact know; it helps prioritise what's built next.
