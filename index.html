Introducing Rostas

What is Rostas?

Rostas is your organisation's volunteer roster assistant. It handles the time-consuming, people-sensitive work of building rosters, collecting availability, and finding replacements when someone can't make it — automatically, fairly, and kindly.

Once set up, Rostas runs most of the roster cycle on its own. Your job shifts from juggling spreadsheets and chasing people by text to simply reviewing what Rostas has prepared and pressing go.

The old wayThe Rostas way
2–3 hours of emails, calls, and chasingUnder 1 hour via a guided 6-step wizard
Flat yes/no availability guesswork3-state availability + 4-tier role preferences
Coordinator manually calls around when someone cancelsAutomated SMS replacement chain handles it automatically
More risk of volunteer burnout from over-rosteringFrequency caps and self-serve opt-outs built in

Getting started: your first roster

Your first roster is the one everyone judges Rostas by. When a first roster looks wrong (people in roles they don't do, or on dates they can't make), the cause is nearly always the setup work before the build, not the build itself. Do these four jobs carefully, in this order, and your first build should be fast and close to right first time.

ℹ️ Roles belong to a roster. A volunteer's roles are set separately for each roster they serve on, so the roster and its roles need to exist first. If you haven't made your roster yet, do Set up a new roster before step 3 below.

1. Get your volunteers into Rostas

Only a name is required, but add mobile numbers and emails wherever you have them: they're how volunteers get their roster, availability requests and replacement asks. Pick the row that matches where your list lives now.

Your list is…What to do
Handwritten (or in your head)Type it into a new Google Sheet, one person per row, with the columns Name, Mobile and Email (and Roster if you like). Then import it. For just a handful of people, add them one at a time instead.
An Excel fileImport it. Rostas takes .xlsx, .xls and .csv files up to 5 MB. Make sure it has one person per row before you start. Or, to keep editing your list and have Rostas follow it, upload it to Google Drive, choose File → Save as Google Sheets, and connect it (next row).
Just the contacts on your phoneNo spreadsheet needed — export your phone's contacts as a vCard file and import it directly. Good for a quick way to try Rostas out, or for a small organisation with no other volunteer list.
Infoodle, Planning Center or a Google SheetAll three can be connected instead of imported: see Choosing your volunteer database, and Google Sheet for a sheet. Names, mobiles and emails then stay up to date every night, so you never keep two lists.
A CSV export from another databaseImport it. Rostas matches the columns it recognises; check each match before you import, and ignore any columns you don't need.

When you're done, open Profiles and check the number of people matches your list. If your file said which roster each person serves on, map that column during the import and they're put on their rosters for you. Otherwise click Set roster types now at the end of the import.

2. Make two decisions

Decide these before you open any profiles, because they decide how much of the profile work is yours.

DecisionChoose this……if
Who chooses each volunteer's roles?You set themYou know your people well. It's the fastest and most accurate route: you do it in step 3 below.
Ask the volunteersYou don't know who does what, or the team is large. Rostas sends each volunteer one message asking which roles suit them, at Step 3 — Role Prefs of your first roster cycle. You can mix the two: set the people you're sure of, then send only to volunteers with no roles set.
Will you ask for availability?Send an availability pollRecommended for a first roster. Each volunteer marks the dates they can't do, at Step 4 — Availability, so nobody is rostered on a date they're away.
Build with everyone availableYour team is small and you'll fix clashes on the Board yourself. Everyone is treated as free on every date.

3. Work through each profile, one by one

This is the step that makes the first roster look right. If you know your people, set each profile up yourself in one sitting:

  1. Open Profiles and pick the roster with the pills at the top.
  2. Click the first volunteer.
  3. Check their mobile and email, and the Active, Email and SMS toggles (what each one does).
  4. Set each of their roles to Does This, Fills In or Doesn't Do (Roles).
  5. If they serve less often than every week, set their Frequency. If they should always (or never) serve with someone, set Relational rostering.
  6. Press the › arrow at the top of the profile, beside the search bar, to go straight to the next volunteer. Repeat until you reach the end.
⚠️ A volunteer with no roles is left out of the build. Rostas has nothing to place them in. Before you build, use the No roles set button on the Profiles page: it shows how many are left and steps through only those people.

4. Check, then build

  1. Open Insights for the roster. Any role with fewer People than it needs shows up here, before the build rather than after it.
  2. Start your first roster cycle. Follow the two decisions you made in step 2 at Role Prefs and Availability.
  3. Check the result on the Board before you send it. Your copy is still in test mode, so nothing reaches a volunteer until you turn test mode off at Step 6 — Notify.

How Rostas Works — The Big Picture

Rostas works in a rhythm that repeats every few weeks. The six-step roster cycle guides you through each stage in order:

StepNameWhat happens
1Confirm DatesYou confirm which dates this build covers. Public and school holidays are already marked, and you can drop a date or add a one-off one.
2RolesYou check which roles are needed for this build and how many people each needs. They start as they were last time.
3Role PrefsVolunteers (or the coordinator) set which roles each volunteer is willing to do: "Yes, I do this", "Happy to fill in" or "Not for me". Set once, remembered for every future build, and editable on Profile pages. Often skipped.
4AvailabilityRostas asks each volunteer which upcoming dates they can do, by the Rostas app, email or text. Or you can skip the poll and build with everyone available.
5Build & CheckThe engine fills every slot it can, then the roster opens on the Board so you can check it, make swaps or replacements, and refill or rebuild if needed.
6NotifyRostas tells every rostered volunteer their assignments, by the app, email or text. Automatic reminders follow closer to the date, with no further action needed.

Key Concepts

Availability — Simple for Volunteers

Before each build, Rostas can email your volunteers asking which upcoming dates they're available for. Volunteers receive a personalised link and see a simple calendar of dates. Everything starts as Available — volunteers only change the dates they can't make.

✅ Available
The default. Volunteer is free and happy to serve on this date.
❓ Not sure
Volunteer might be available. Rostas may provisionally assign them.
❌ Can't make it
Volunteer is unavailable. They won't be assigned to this date.
💡 If a volunteer doesn't respond, click Nudge in the roster cycle. Rostas reminds them by app or email straight away, and texts anyone still silent two hours later. Nothing is texted unless you nudge. See Step 4 — Availability.

The Intelligent Matching Engine

The roster engine doesn't fill slots blindly. It uses a four-tier priority system that respects both when volunteers can serve and what they actually want to do:

TierAvailabilityRole matchPriority
1✅ AvailablePrimary role (Yes, I do this)Highest — assigned first
2✅ AvailableFillable role (Happy to fill in)Second choice
3❓ Not surePrimary roleThird — provisional
4❓ Not sureFillable roleLast resort

Within each tier, Rostas picks the volunteer who has served least in recent weeks (counting all their roles), then whoever did this particular role longest ago — keeping things fair automatically over time without any manual tracking.

The Roster Board

The Board is your day-to-day view of the current roster. It shows every slot, every date, and every volunteer assignment at a glance. A traffic light colour system tells you instantly what's filled, what's pending, and what needs attention.

🟢 Filled
Rostered, and they marked themselves available. (Confirming is separate: the volunteer taps “Yes, I'll be there”.)
🟡 Pending
Provisionally assigned (volunteer marked Not sure). Monitor for confirmation.
🔵 SMS Chain
The replacement chain is running — Rostas is asking backup volunteers. The card says “In chain”.
🔴 Unfilled
No suitable volunteer found automatically. Needs your attention.

The Automated SMS Replacement Chain

When a rostered volunteer can't make it, they tap Can't make it in their roster email, their reminder, or the app. Rostas immediately starts working through the backup list — no coordinator involvement needed.

🌙 Quiet hours are always respected. Rostas never sends automated SMS during your quiet hours. If the replacement chain reaches a volunteer during that window, it pauses and resumes automatically once quiet hours end. You set the start and end times in Settings → Timing & behaviour (the default is 9pm–8am).

Every reply-seeking message — the automated chain above, Manual Replacement, and a Cross-Roster Ask — decides how to reach the volunteer the same way, in this order:

TierConditionWhat the volunteer gets
1 — App pushHas the Rostas app installed with notifications onA push notification straight to their phone — tap it, answer Yes/No in the app. No code, no SMS.
2 — SMS linkNo push notification receivedA short SMS carrying a direct personalised link — no app, no code, nothing to type.
💡 You can see and edit the wording for every tier — including the app-push text and the SMS — in Settings: text wording is under SMS, app-notification wording under App, and email wording under Email wording.

The Tier 2 message contains a personalised link straight to the volunteer's accept / decline page. There's nothing to type and no separate entry page to visit — they just tap the link in the text.

Looking After Your Volunteers

Rostas is designed with your volunteers in mind, not just your roster.

FeatureWhat it does for your volunteers
Frequency capsSet a maximum number of times each volunteer can be rostered per number of weeks. The engine never exceeds this, even if they're always available.
Quiet hoursNo automated SMS during the quiet window you set in Settings (default 9pm–8am). Volunteers are never disturbed at night.
Self-serve opt-outVolunteers can pause or opt out of SMS and email at any time using a link in any Rostas message — no need to contact you.  They may be happy just to print out their roster. 
Relational rosteringRostas tries to schedule family members and preferred buddies on the same dates — making it easier to serve together or share transport. 

Who Does What

Rostas is designed so that most of the routine work happens automatically. Here's how responsibilities divide between you, your volunteers, and Rostas:

WhoWhat they do
You — the coordinatorRun the 6-step wizard each cycle, review the built roster, handle exceptions, manage volunteers and settings
Your volunteersRespond to availability emails, set role preferences, accept or decline replacement requests by tapping the link in a text or email, or answering in the app.
RostasEverything in between — building the roster, sending notifications, chasing replacements, sending reminders, enforcing frequency caps
✨ Rostas doesn't replace your judgement. There will be times when you want to override what Rostas has built — swap two people, add someone manually, or put a slot on hold. The Board makes all of this easy. Rostas handles the routine so you can focus on the exceptions.

Setting up your copy

Rostas runs in your own copy of the Rostas spreadsheet, in your Google account. Straight after you sign up, your welcome page links to a short video guide that shows every click. These are the same steps in writing, for anyone who prefers to read or needs them again later. It's easiest on a computer, not a phone.

1. Make your copy

Click Make your copy of Rostas on your welcome page (or in the video guide), then Make a copy. Your own copy of the spreadsheet opens in a new tab.

2. Let Rostas keep your copy up to date (optional)

In your new sheet, click Share (top right). Type rostasapp@gmail.com, choose Editor, and click Send. This lets Rostas send your copy fixes and new features, the same as every other church. Your copy then finishes setting each update up by itself, so once setup is done you never need to open the script editor.

ℹ️ This step is your choice. Editor access lets Rostas open your sheet, and your sheet holds your volunteers' names and contact details. We only use it to update your copy of Rostas, never to look at, copy or change your volunteer or roster information. If you'd rather not give that access, simply skip this step: your copy keeps working just as it is, with every feature it has today. It won't get fixes and new features until you share it.

You can also share only when you need to. When we let you know that an important update is ready, share your sheet as above, wait until we confirm your copy is updated, then remove the access: click Share, open the menu beside rostasapp@gmail.com and choose Remove access.

3. Open the script editor

In your sheet, open the Extensions menu and choose Apps Script. It opens in another tab. You won't need to touch any code.

4. Deploy Rostas

At the top right, click Deploy, then New deployment. Click the cog beside “Select type” and choose Web app. Set “Execute as” to Me and “Who has access” to Anyone, then click Deploy.

5. Get past Google's warning

Click the blue Authorise access button and choose your Google account. Google then says it “hasn't verified this app”. It says this about every new copy of Rostas, because the copy you just made lives in your own Google account and Google hasn't reviewed it. Rostas is made by Rostas in Waitara, New Zealand. Click the small Advanced link at the bottom left.

A new line appears underneath. Click Go to… (unsafe).

If your church uses Google Workspace and you see “This app is blocked” instead, your Workspace administrator needs to allow it, or you can set Rostas up with an ordinary Gmail account.

6. Choose what to share with Rostas

A sign-in screen appears. Click Continue. The next screen lists what your copy of Rostas needs. Tick Select all at the top of the list, scroll to the bottom and click Continue. If you skip Select all, parts of Rostas will not work later.

“Send email on your behalf” is how Rostas sends roster reminders from your account. It can't read your email. Your rosters and volunteer list stay in your own Google Sheet (see the privacy policy).

7. Copy your Web app URL

The deployment window now shows a Deployment ID and, below it, a Web app URL. You need the Web app URL. Click the small Copy button beside it, not your browser's address bar. Save the link somewhere safe as well.

8. Open Rostas

Open a new browser tab, paste the link and press Enter. The Rostas Setup Wizard starts (see The Setup Wizard below). Bookmark this page now (Ctrl+D, or ⌘+D on a Mac), because you will open it often.

If Google says “Sorry, unable to open the file”, your browser is signed in to more than one Google account. Open the link in an Incognito window (Ctrl+Shift+N, or ⌘+Shift+N on a Mac) instead; you don't need to sign in. For everyday use, see Put Rostas on your desktop.

💡 Lost the link? Open your sheet, then Extensions → Apps Script → Deploy → Manage deployments, and copy the Web app URL again.

When you open Rostas for the first time, the setup wizard runs automatically. It takes about five minutes and covers the essentials — your organisation name, your contact details, and a quick SMS test to make sure everything is working before your first roster build.

The wizard runs once. After it's complete, Rostas opens directly to the Builds dashboard on every future visit.

ℹ️ Before you start: have your Rostas licence key ready — it's in your Rostas welcome email. If someone else at your organisation has already set Rostas up, you don't need this wizard: ask them to forward you the coordinator link (see Your coordinator link).
⚠️ Signed in to more than one Google account? If your browser is signed in to two Google accounts at once (for example your personal Gmail and the church account), the setup page won't open: you'll see "Sorry, unable to open the file at this time". This is a Google limitation, not a fault in your copy. The simplest fix is to make a Church browser profile with only the church account in it, and do the setup there. It takes two minutes. See Put Rostas on your desktop. For a quick one-off, you can also open the setup link in a private window (Chrome: Ctrl+Shift+N on Windows, ⌘+Shift+N on a Mac).

The Setup Wizard

The wizard has five steps — Licence, Organisation, Your Details, SMS and Done — shown across the top of the screen. You can see at a glance how far through you are and what's coming next.

Step 1 — Licence

Enter the licence key from your Rostas welcome email (it looks like RSTS-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX; you can also find it in your account at rostas.app) and click Validate →. Rostas checks it and moves you on to your organisation's details. If the key isn't accepted, check it for typos, then contact support@rostas.app.

Step 2 — Your Organisation

The first step establishes your organisation's identity. The name you enter here appears in every message sent to volunteers — availability emails, roster notifications, and SMS reminders.

Click Next → when all required fields are filled. The button stays disabled until everything is valid.

Step 3 — Your Details

Step 3 captures the primary coordinator's contact details. These are used for the SMS test send, error alerts, and the bottom of the coordinator app sidebar.

FieldWhat to enter

Step 4 — SMS Test

Before your first roster build, Rostas verifies that SMS is working. This step sends a real test message to your mobile so you can confirm everything is set up correctly.

How to complete Step 4

Your mobile number is pre-filled from Step 3. Tap Send test SMS → and wait for the message to arrive on your phone — usually within 30 seconds. Once received, a green confirmation appears and the Next button enables.

💡 If the test message doesn't arrive within a minute, check that your mobile number is correct. You can edit the number in the field and try again. If it still fails, email support@rostas.app — SMS sending is set up by Rostas, not something you configure yourself.
ℹ️ If SMS isn't set up on your copy yet, Step 4 shows a "SMS isn't set up yet" screen instead of the test send, with a link to support@rostas.app and a Continue without SMS button. You can finish setup and start using Rostas by email — SMS replacement requests and reminders begin working once SMS is switched on.  Your first 20 SMS credits are free; after that, texts use prepaid credit packs (see SMS Credits).

Step 5 — Done

Once SMS is confirmed, the wizard completes and Rostas is ready to use.

From here you have two options:

  • Go to Build → — takes you straight to the Builds dashboard, ready to start your first roster cycle. This is the right choice if your volunteers are already imported.
  • Explore Settings — opens the Settings view if you want to review or customise anything before you import volunteers or do your first build.

Your coordinator link

When set-up finishes, Rostas emails you a coordinator link (to the Organisation email, or your own email if that's blank). This link is how you open Rostas from now on — bookmark it on every computer you use. It works like a password for the coordinator screens, so don't forward it to volunteers or post it anywhere.

If you open Rostas without it, you'll see a Coordinator sign-in box: paste your coordinator link there, or click Email me the coordinator link to have it sent again. A device you've signed in on remembers you, so this is usually only needed once per device.

Put Rostas on your desktop

Most coordinators use Rostas on a computer that's also signed in to their personal Google account. Google can't open Rostas when a browser has two Google accounts signed in, so the best setup is a separate Church profile in Chrome with only the church account in it, and a Rostas icon that always opens there. You do this once, and from then on you just click the icon.

  2. Click your profile picture at the top right, just left of the three dots (if you have no picture, it's a small round person outline).
  3. Click Add (some versions say Add Chrome profile).
  4. In the new window, click Sign in and sign in with the church Google account only, the one that owns your copy of Rostas. Don't add any other accounts to this profile.
  5. If Chrome asks "Turn on sync?", either answer is fine.
  6. Name the profile Church and pick a different colour, so you can tell its windows apart, then click Done.

  2. Click the three dots (top right) → Cast, save and share → Install page as app…
  3. Name it Rostas and click Install. Rostas opens in its own window, with the Rostas R as its icon.
ComputerWhere the Rostas icon goes

Importing your volunteers from CSV

The quickest way to get your volunteers into Rostas is to import a spreadsheet — from a previous roster system, a church database, or even a simple contact list. This is how most organisations start. If your volunteers already live in another system, or in a Google Sheet you keep up to date, you can sync them automatically instead — see the Connector Appendix.

  1. Open Profiles and click Import Volunteer CSV on the toolbar.
  2. Click Choose a file and pick your spreadsheet, or drag the file onto the page.
  3. Click Continue to matching.
  4. Check how each of your columns has been matched, and change any that look wrong.
  5. Click Preview [N] volunteers and look over the list.
  6. Click Check for duplicates.
  7. If anyone is already in Rostas, choose Skip duplicates (recommended) or Update existing records.
  8. Click Import [N] volunteers. A summary shows how many were added. They're in the Profiles list straight away.
  9. Click Set roster types now to put the new volunteers on their rosters, or View all volunteers to do it later.
💡 Only a name is required. Include mobile numbers and emails if you have them, so volunteers can get texts, availability requests and notifications. If your spreadsheet has a column for roster type, map it during the import and volunteers are assigned to the right rosters automatically. Phone numbers missing a country code are corrected for you.

Importing a list of volunteers from your phone's contacts

If you don't have a spreadsheet to import — or you just want a quick way to try Rostas out — you can import straight from your phone's Contacts app. Rostas reads a vCard file (.vcf), the standard format every phone uses to export contacts. It's the same Import Wizard as a CSV, just a different file type in step 1.

  1. Export your contacts as a vCard file.
    Your phoneHow to export
    iPhoneOpen icloud.com/contacts in a browser and sign in. Select all your contacts, click the ⚙️ gear in the bottom-left corner, then choose Export vCard. One .vcf file downloads with everyone in it.

Frequency — how often they serve

The Frequency control — labelled ONCE EVERY — caps how often this volunteer is rostered in this roster, so nobody is over-used even when they're always available. Tap a pill: once every 1wk, 2wk, and so on up to 12wk. If none is set, Rostas uses once every 4 weeks.

ℹ️ The cap counts distinct dates, not roles. A volunteer rostered to two roles on the same day counts as one date — so serving twice in one Sunday doesn't use up two of their allocation.

Relational rostering — serving together

Relational rostering asks Rostas to place people together on the same dates — a Buddy they like to serve alongside, or a Household so family members share the same rota. Turn on "Serves With" and pick who, then choose how closely they should be linked: the same date, or the same role too.

👥 Who you can pick. The Buddy list shows everyone on this roster. The Household list puts the likely family first, under "Same surname or household" (the same surname, or the same household in Infoodle or Planning Center), then everyone else under "Everyone else on this roster". So a couple or blended family with different surnames can still be linked as a household.
🔁 Their frequencies need to line up. Rostas can only keep a pair together when they serve equally often, or one serves a multiple of the other's frequency (say every 2 weeks and every 4 weeks: they serve together on the 4-weekly person's turns, and the other also serves alone in between). Combinations like 2 and 3 weeks, or 4 and 6, would rarely meet, so the profile won't accept them. If you pick someone like that, or change the frequency to one that doesn't fit, the choice goes back to what it was and a red message explains why, with a Match button that sets this person to their partner's frequency and makes the link. If one of the pair has been away, Rostas may hold the other back for a turn so they get back in step and serve together again.
📌 Two ways to serve together. Left unticked, a Buddy or Household pair just needs to be rostered on the same date — any role each of them is capable of is fine. This suits people who want to travel in together or simply be there on the same Sunday. Tick "Same role together" when one person is mentoring or training the other — this asks Rostas to put them on the same role, together, every time. Because that's a tighter constraint, a same-role pair is held back more often, and only placed once a slot exists that fits them both.
ℹ️ Relational rostering is not the same as role linking. Role linking ties two roles together for the same person; relational rostering places two people on the same dates. One is about jobs, the other about company.

Reordering roles

Some shortfalls aren't about headcount at all — they're about fill order. Rostas fills roles top-to-bottom in the order they're listed on Adding and changing roles: once a volunteer is placed in one role for a date, they're not considered for any role further down the list that same date. A role near the bottom can end up short simply because every volunteer who could have filled it was claimed by an earlier role first.

Where moving a short role earlier in that order would help, its card shows a distinct suggestion:

⚠️ Always a trade, never free capacity. Moving a role earlier redistributes an existing shared volunteer — it doesn't create a new one. ℹ️ Insights tells you, it doesn't do it for you. Every suggestion on this page is informational. Set up a new roster

Run this wizard when you're setting up a roster type for the first time — for example, adding a Music roster or a Hospitality team. It takes about 10 minutes and walks you through five steps: Name & Icon, Roles, Season, Volunteers, and Review.

ℹ️ You only set up a roster once per roster type. After setup is complete, every future build is a roster cycle instead.

Step 1 — Name & Icon

Give your roster a short name and choose an icon. The name appears in SMS messages, emails, and the coordinator app — so keep it brief and clear. A few words are kept for Rostas's own settings and can't be used as a roster name: Name, Email, Phone, ID, Key and Role.

Step 2 — Roles

Define the roles that need to be filled each time this roster runs. For each role, set a minimum and maximum number of people required, and, if you like, the time it Starts. You can add, rename, or retire roles later from Adding and changing roles.

🕑 Role times are optional. On a roster where roles start at different times (a marae day with the kitchen at 7am and the karanga at 10am, or Morning Tea at 11am), choose each role's Starts time. It then shows after the role name in the Roster Board's green role bars, on the volunteer's roster in the app and in emails, e.g. "Serving Morning Tea · 11am". Text messages leave it out to stay within one text. Leave it on No time and nothing extra is shown. You can set the same times later on the roster cycle's Roles step or in Manage roles.
💡 Role names matter. Use clear, specific names that volunteers will recognise — e.g. "Sound Desk", "Welcome Team", "Data Projection". These names appear in SMS messages and emails sent to volunteers.

Step 3 — Season

A season defines the date range, frequency, and day of week for this roster. Rostas uses seasons to calculate which dates to roster for, and to overlay public and school holidays automatically.

Step 4 — Volunteers

Select which volunteers from your list will serve on this roster. Tick each volunteer you want to include. Only volunteers already in Rostas appear here — if someone is missing, add them in Profiles first then return to this step.

Step 5 — Review

The final step shows a summary of everything you've set up — roster name, icon, roles, season dates, and volunteer count. Review it carefully before confirming.

Click Create Roster → to finish. Rostas takes you straight into the roster cycle for your new roster, ready for its first build, and the roster appears on the Builds dashboard.

Run a roster cycle

The roster cycle is what you run every few weeks. Each roster type has its own (the page is headed "Run the [your roster] roster cycle") and it takes you through the same six steps every time: Confirm Dates, Roles, Role Prefs, Availability, Build & Check, and Notify.

To start, open the Builds dashboard and click a roster's row or state button. If a build is already under way, you're taken straight back to the step you left off at.

Finding your way around the wizard

Across the top of every step is a row of six circles, one per step. Each says how that step stands: Completed, Skipped, In progress, Ready or Pending. Click a completed or skipped circle to look at that step again; click Confirm Dates at any time to go back to the dates.

Directly under the circles is one bar that stays in the same place on every step: ← Back on the left, "Step N of 6" in the middle, and the main button (for example Continue to Role Prefs →) on the right. Some steps have their own buttons inside the card instead, and then the right-hand button is hidden.

Step 1 — Confirm Dates

The wizard opens on the Build Dates screen, where you confirm which dates this build covers. A small box at the top reminds you how it works: clicking a date removes it from the build, and clicking the blue arrow makes the build longer or shorter. You can also add a one-off date with + Add Date. All of this is explained in Choosing which dates to build below.

When the dates look right, click Continue to Roles → on the Build Dates title row. That saves the dates and takes you to the next step.

Step 2 — Roles

This step shows one card, Edit Roles: every role on this roster with its name, its Min and Max headcount, the time it Starts, and an Active tick box. You can rename a role, change its headcount, set or change its start time, untick it to leave it out, or use + Add a role. What you set here carries forward to later builds. Roles start out as they were last time, so on a routine build you can usually just click Continue to Role Prefs →.

There's no separate Save button: any change you make is saved when you leave the step, whether by Back, Continue, or clicking a circle at the top.

ℹ️ Changing the order roles are filled in? Do that from Manage roles on the ⋯ menu.

Step 3 — Role Prefs

The two "send only…" and "set them myself" options only appear when some volunteers have no roles. Each volunteer gets one message: through the Rostas app if they have it, otherwise by email, otherwise by text.

After you send, the step shows how the messages went (for example "Role notifications sent to 24 (18 by app, 5 by email, 1 by text)"), with a count of who has Replied and who has Not yet. Use Remind those who have not replied if you want to give people a nudge, and Continue to availability when you're ready to move on. You don't have to wait for every reply before moving on.

💡 Changed your mind after skipping? Click the Role Prefs circle at the top. A skipped step shows the same options again, and choosing one replaces the skip.

Step 4 — Availability

To send the poll:

  1. Choose Send an availability poll.
  2. Click Send the poll. Sending takes about a minute.
  3. Watch the Responded and Not yet counts as replies come in. The step also says how the poll went out (for example "Availability requests sent to 30 (30 by email)").
  4. To chase the rest, click Nudge the [N] who have not answered. The nudge goes by the app or email straight away. Anyone still silent two hours later gets one text (never during quiet hours).
  5. When you're ready, click Continue to Build Roster.
⏰ Give people a few days to reply before you build. You can leave the wizard and come back; the roster shows Gathering on the Builds dashboard in the meantime.

Step 5 — Build & Check

This step asks Ready to build roster? If some volunteers still haven't answered the poll, you can nudge them first and come back later. Otherwise choose Build as is → Build roster. Anyone who hasn't replied counts as available.

The roster engine looks at every volunteer's availability, role preferences, how recently they've served and how often they want to serve, and fills every slot it can (see The Intelligent Matching Engine). A build can take up to 5–10 minutes for a big roster. When it's finished, the Roster Board opens by itself.

⚠️ If a build can't complete (for example the roster is paused, your Rostas subscription has lapsed, or a role or date setting blocks it), the step says "Last build attempt failed" with the reason, and a Try Again button for once it's fixed.

When the roster looks right:

Step 6 — Notify

After you send from the Board, the wizard opens on the Notify step. While messages are going out it shows "Sent N of M so far". You can leave the page and sending carries on by itself. You can look at the Board meanwhile (Look at the Roster Board), but changes there are paused until sending finishes. The Notify circle reads "Sending…" until it's done.

Each volunteer gets one message with their roles and dates and confirm and decline links: through the Rostas app if they have it, otherwise by email, otherwise by text. When it's done, the step shows when notifications were sent and how many people were told and how (for example "Roster sent to 29 volunteers (29 by email)"). It also tells you about anyone who wasn't reached:

  • Text-only volunteers during quiet hours: their text waits until quiet hours end.
  • Couldn't be reached just now: the message didn't go through.
  • No app, email or phone number: they can't be told; add their details on their profile.

If anyone can still be reached, a Send to the N not yet reached button tries them again. When you're ready to start the next roster, click Start Next Cycle.

📬 What happens automatically after Step 6:
  • Reminder email sent 3 days before each rostered date (change this in Settings → Timing & behaviour → Reminder days before service)
  • The day before, an app notification or a text, depending on Settings → Timing & behaviour → Day-before reminder texts
  • If a volunteer taps "Can't make it", the SMS replacement chain starts automatically
No further action needed until the next roster cycle.

Pausing, re-dating or removing a roster (the ⋯ menu)

The ⋯ menu beside each row's state button on the Builds dashboard holds everything you do with a roster between builds — pausing it, adjusting its dates or roles, changing its season length, clearing its data, or deleting it outright. Click it to open a pop-up of options.

OptionWhat it does

Manage DatesOpens the Build Dates screen on its own, so you can adjust which dates this roster runs on — remove one, shift the build boundary, or add a one-off extra date. See Choosing which dates to build below. Changes apply from the next build onward — dates already built are never changed retroactively.
Manage rolesOpens the roles screen for this roster — add, rename, set headcounts, or deactivate roles. See Adding and changing roles below. Also applies from the next build onward.

Pause / Resume roster

Pausing a roster stops all automatic activity for it straight away — no builds, no reminder emails or texts, no availability emails or nudges, and no replacement chains (a chain already running stops asking people) — without losing any history. Anything that falls due while it's paused is skipped, not saved up for later. The menu option and the row's state button both switch to Resume roster while paused; click either to pick back up exactly where you left off.

Choosing which dates to build

Before each build you confirm exactly which dates it covers, on the Build Dates screen. It lays out your roster's dates in three bands — what's already built, what this build will cover, and what's coming next — and lets you drop a date you don't want, shift the boundary between builds, or add a one-off extra date such as Christmas Day or Good Friday.

You reach it two ways: as the first step of a build (Confirm Dates), or on its own from the Builds dashboard — ⋯ — Manage Dates. A small box at the top of the screen sums up the two main controls: clicking a date removes it from the build, and clicking the blue arrow makes the build longer or shorter.

BandWhat it means
Last BuildDates already built in the previous cycle. Each card shows a padlock — these are locked and can't be changed here. If something needs fixing on a date that's already built, do it on the Board instead.
This BuildThe dates the next build will cover — the band header shows a running count (e.g. "This Build · 9 dates"). These are being confirmed right now; clicking Build will roster them.
Next BuildDates coming after this build, also with a running count. Volunteers can already set their availability for these — it's a look-ahead so nothing catches them by surprise.
ExcludedA date you've removed from This Build or Next Build — turned pink so it won't be rostered.

Removing a date

Click any date card in This Build or Next Build to exclude it — the card turns pink and drops out of the build. Click it again to bring the date back. Cards in Last Build can't be removed this way, since they're already built.

Shifting the boundary between builds

A small blue arrow sits on the date at the boundary between This Build and Next Build. Click it to move that date across the line — pulling the next upcoming date into This Build to extend how far this build reaches, or sending the last date of This Build back into Next Build to shorten it.

Adding an extra date

To add a one-off date outside the normal pattern, such as Christmas Eve, Good Friday or a special event:

  1. In the date box under This Build, pick the date.
  2. In the box beside it, type a short label (for example "Good Friday").
  3. Click + Add Date. The new card appears in This Build with your label as a tag, and is included the next time you build.
ℹ️ Holidays are tagged automatically — but tagging and pausing aren't the same thing. Dates that fall directly on a public holiday or a school holiday are labelled for you, and look distinct from dates you've excluded by hand, so you can tell an automatic holiday from a deliberate choice. Long weekends (Labour Weekend, Anzac Weekend, and others) get their own distinct yellow tag too — but the tag alone doesn't pause anything. Only a direct hit on the public holiday or school holiday itself pauses the roster (and only if you've ticked Pause public holidays / Pause school holidays for this roster type). A long-weekend Sunday that isn't itself the public holiday still needs rostering as normal — exclude it by hand if you don't want it covered.
💡 Confirm early. Ideally do this a few weeks before the dates arrive, so there's time to collect availability and build. A perpetual roster rolls forward on its own, so each cycle you're simply confirming the next stretch of dates; a fixed-term roster is confirmed up to its end date. (See Season type for the difference.)
ℹ️ Nothing changes until you save. Removing, shifting, or adding dates only takes effect once you click Continue to Roles → (in the wizard) or Save (when you opened Manage Dates from the ⋯ menu) — leaving without saving discards your changes. When you open this screen standalone via Manage Dates, saved changes apply from the next build onward; dates already built are never altered.

Once a roster exists, you can add, rename, re-size, reorder or retire its roles at any time:

  1. Open Builds.
  2. Click the ⋯ button on the roster's row.
  3. Click Manage roles. The page lists every role with its headcount, its start time and an active switch.
  4. Make your changes (see the table below).
  5. Click Save at the top right.
ActionHow
Add a roleUse the add option at the bottom of the list; enter the name and its minimum and maximum headcount.
Rename a roleUse Rename on the role. The new name appears in future messages to volunteers.
Change headcountAdjust the minimum and maximum for how many people the role needs each time.
Set a start timeChoose the role's time in the Time column (optional). It shows on the Roster Board, in the volunteer app and in emails straight after you save; choose No time to remove it.

Fill order — which role gets first pick

Rostas fills roles top-to-bottom in the order they're listed here — locking each one in fully before moving to the next. If a volunteer is capable of more than one role on a roster, whichever role sits higher in this list gets first claim on them for that date.

Use the Fill Order control on any role — Move to top / Move up / Move down / Move to bottom — to change where it sits. This matters most for a role with a thin, shared bench: putting it higher gives it first pick of your multi-capable volunteers instead of leftovers.

⚠️ Reordering redistributes, it doesn't create capacity. Moving a role up can close its own shortfall, but only by taking a shared volunteer away from whatever role used to get them first. See Insights for exactly which role would carry that cost before you decide.
ℹ️ Changes apply from your next build onward. Renaming a role, changing its headcount, switching it active or inactive, or adding a new role all take effect at the next build — dates you've already built keep their original roles and wording. Nothing changes underneath the volunteers you've already notified.
💡 Deactivate rather than delete. Deactivating a role keeps the full history of who served in it; it just stops appearing in new builds. Reach for deactivate unless you're correcting a genuine setup mistake.
⚠️ Mind the timing when renaming mid-cycle. Because a rename only affects messages sent after it, volunteers already notified will have the old name in their earlier email or text. If you rename partway through a cycle, expect a brief mismatch until the next build.

How often a roster runs

There are two separate frequency ideas in Rostas: how often the roster runs, and how often each volunteer serves. This section covers the roster side — its cadence and whether the season is fixed or ongoing. The per-volunteer cap is set on each person's profile (see Frequency on the Profile detail page); the two are explained together at the end.

Roster cadence — how often the roster runs

Not every roster runs weekly. A community meal might run fortnightly, a kids' ministry only on the 1st and 3rd Sunday, a review team monthly. The Roster Frequency setting on the Season Length page sets this cadence per roster type — it decides which dates appear in the season, and therefore which dates Rostas builds for. You reach it two ways: when you first create a roster (the Season step of Set up a new roster), or any time afterwards from the Builds dashboard — ⋯ — Season Length.

OptionWhen to use it
DailyRoster runs every day it applies — e.g. a hospital-visit or meals roster
WeeklyRoster runs every week — e.g. Sunday service
FortnightlyRoster runs every two weeks
1st and 3rdRuns on the 1st and 3rd occurrence of the day each month
2nd and 4thRuns on the 2nd and 4th occurrence of the day each month
MonthlyRuns once a month — e.g. a monthly working bee
💡 Cadence is set once per roster type and applies to everyone in it. If two groups run on different weeks, set them up as separate roster types.

Season type — fixed-term or perpetual

Below the cadence pills, the Season Type control decides whether this roster has an end or simply keeps going:

TypeWhat it means
Fixed-termThe season has a real start and end date. Use it for anything with a natural finish — a school term, a set programme, a holiday series.
Perpetual (ongoing)The roster runs indefinitely with no end date to manage. Rostas keeps enough weeks generated ahead automatically, so a perpetual roster never runs out of dates.
ℹ️ Perpetual seasons roll forward on their own — the end date is maintained by Rostas, so you never have to extend them. Most ongoing church rosters (Sunday service, welcome team) suit Perpetual. If you later need a perpetual roster to stop on a set date, switch it to Fixed-term and give it an end date.

Volunteer frequency cap — how often a person serves

Separate from the roster's cadence, each volunteer has their own cap — how often they personally get rostered — so nobody is used every week just because they're always free. You set it on each person's profile (Frequency on the Profile detail page), independently for each roster they serve in. The two settings work together:

💡 The cap counts distinct dates, not individual roles. A volunteer in two roles on the same date counts as one date — so serving twice on one Sunday doesn't use two of their allocation.

Clearing a roster's test data

Reset roster data clears a roster type's operational history — built assignments, availability responses, replacement chains — without touching the roster's setup: its roles, volunteers, and season stay exactly as they are.

  1. Open Builds.
  2. Click the ⋯ button on the roster's row.
  3. Click Reset roster data.
  4. Read the list of what will be cleared.
  5. Click Reset "[roster]" data. (Click Cancel to keep everything.) The roster's state button now reads First Setup, ready for a fresh first build. A paused roster stays Paused.
💡 Built for getting started. This is the tool for wiping sample or test data before a roster goes live — clear out anything built while you were setting up, then start rostering for real without deleting and re-creating the whole roster type.
⚠️ Reset vs Delete. Reset clears history but keeps the roster type itself — its roles and volunteer assignments to it survive. Delete removes the roster type entirely — see Deleting a Roster below. If you're clearing out test data before going live, Reset is almost always what you want.

Deleting a Roster

Delete roster permanently removes the roster type — its shell, roles, seasons, build history, and every assignment tied to it. Unlike Reset, there's no going back.

  1. Open Builds.
  2. Click the ⋯ button on the roster's row.
  3. Click Delete roster.
  4. Read the list of what will be deleted. If you only need a break, click Cancel and choose Pause roster instead.
  5. Click Delete "[roster]" roster.
⚠️ Deleting is permanent. If you only need to stop a roster for a while — over summer, say — use Pause from the ⋯ menu instead. Pausing keeps all the roster's history and settings; deleting removes them for good.

The Roster Board is where you manage the roster between builds. You'll come here to check on replacement progress, make manual swaps, confirm pending assignments, or search for a specific volunteer. Access it by clicking Boards in the sidebar.

Printing the roster

To print the roster for a noticeboard or to hand out:

  1. Click Print / save as PDF under the search box on the Board. The roster you're looking at opens in a new tab: every date as a row and every role as a column, with everyone's names. Weeks with no service say so, and people who declined are left out.
  2. Click Print / save as PDF at the top of the new tab.
  3. Choose your printer and print. It prints on A4 landscape, with the column headings repeated on every page. To make a PDF instead, choose Save as PDF as the printer.

Seeing a card's connected cards

When you click a card, Rostas also shows you what it's connected to on that date. Each connected card's border turns bold in its own colour (green on a Filled card) and pulses three times:

  • The same person in another role. If the volunteer is also rostered in a linked role that day, that card lights up.
  • Their buddy or household partner. If they're paired with someone who is serving that day too, the partner's card lights up.

If a connected card is off to the side, the Board scrolls sideways to bring it into view. The highlight clears when you click another card, click empty space on the Board, or click Close.

Saving someone as declined

When a volunteer tells you they can't make it:

  1. Click their card on the Board.
  2. Click Save as declined. A confirm step opens under the action bar.
  3. Leave Let [Name] know they're no longer rostered ticked, or untick it if you've already spoken to them.
  4. Click Save as declined again. (Click Cancel to change nothing.)

What happens next:

  • If that date's roster has already gone out and the box was ticked, Rostas tells them: an app notification, or a text with a link to their roster if they don't have the app.
  • If the roster for that date hasn't gone out yet, nothing is sent and the Board tells you so. They simply won't be on the roster when it's sent.
  • Rostas then starts the SMS replacement chain to find someone to cover. If the roster is paused, the card waits for you instead.

When the roster changes while the Board is open

Volunteers answer texts and emails at any time, and other coordinators may be working on the same roster, so the Board keeps itself up to date:

  • It refreshes by itself. Every 30 seconds, and whenever you come back to the tab, the Board checks for changes (a volunteer's Yes, a decline, another coordinator's change) and redraws in place.
  • If you have a card open, it doesn't redraw under you. A bar at the top says "The roster has changed since you opened the Board" with a Reload button.
  • Each card is checked when you click it. If it changed since the Board loaded, a red note says what it shows now and the buttons are greyed out; reload the Board first. If Rostas is in the middle of asking someone to cover it, a yellow note says who and when their reply is due.
  • Saves never undo a volunteer's answer. If someone said Yes to that slot after your Board loaded, your change is refused and the Board reloads so you can see who's on it now.

The coloured messages the Board shows after a save stay up for 10 seconds.

Swapping two volunteers

When two rostered volunteers want to trade dates:

  1. Click the first volunteer's card on the Board.
  2. Click Swap with…. The card gets a gold border while Rostas waits for the second card. (Click Cancel in the bar to stop.)

Understanding your roster's rhythm

Once warnings are clear, the rest of the page gives you a different kind of visibility — not problems to fix, but patterns to understand. Start with the three charts at the top:

  • Replacement Requests (latest 4) — a card for each of the four most recent requests, newest first, however long ago they started. Each card says who couldn't make it ("Hannah Tane can't make it for Door & Offering on Sun 15 Nov"), then a bar of how the people asked to cover it answered: accepted (green), declined (red), didn't reply in time (amber) and still being asked (grey). Underneath, it says how the request ended:
    • Replaced by [name]: that volunteer said yes.
    • Replaced on the Board by [name]: you picked someone yourself on the Roster Board.
    • Still in progress: Rostas is still asking. "Nobody asked yet" means the first ask hasn't gone out yet.
    • Ended without a replacement: everyone suitable was asked, the date has passed, or a newer request started for the same slot.
    Lots of amber across the cards can mean your replacement timers are too short for how quickly your volunteers reply, or that quiet hours leave too small a sending window.
  • Communications Sent (7 days) — how many messages went to volunteers each day: emails (navy), texts (gold) and app notifications (green). An app notification counts as App only when it was delivered; if it fell back to a text, it counts as SMS. Next to the key it says how many volunteers have the app with notifications on, so an empty green part can simply mean nobody needed a notification that week.
  • Activity (7 days) — one bar per day. The number is how many log entries Rostas wrote that day. The colour is the worst thing that happened: green for no problems, amber for at least one warning, red for at least one error. For a red or amber day, pick that day in Message Logs to see what went wrong.

Below the charts, two things are worth checking regularly:

  • Message Logs — a table of what Rostas did today, oldest first: every reminder, replacement ask, swap and roster message, with who it went to, how it was sent and how they answered. Use the date picker to look at an earlier day, and tick Show system events too to include behind-the-scenes events such as volunteer syncs and roster builds. Click Key: what the colours and labels mean above the table for a full explanation. In short: a pale red row means something failed and a pale yellow row is a warning (both always shown); in the Response column, green means the volunteer said yes, gold means still waiting, amber means no reply in time, and red means declined or not sent. This is your first-stop diagnostic: if a volunteer says they didn't get a message, pick that day and look for them.
  • Communication Failures — the most direct troubleshooting tool, shown in the Warnings column. When all is well it reads "No comms failures"; otherwise it lists recent send errors and warnings. If a volunteer tells you they never received a replacement SMS, check here for an entry on the date in question. If you see nothing at all for that date, the message may have been suppressed before sending — check SMS enabled, test mode, and quiet hours.

Texts and their wording

Three settings here are worth understanding before you go live.

⚠️

SMS enabled (Quick Controls) — the master kill switch. When this is false, no SMS leaves Rostas at all — not replacements, not reminders, not test sends. The System Status card shows a red warning when this is off. Only disable it deliberately (e.g. during a migration or if you need to pause sending temporarily).

⚠️

SMS test mode (Quick Controls) — when true, every SMS that would go to a volunteer is redirected to the coordinator mobile instead. The message is prefixed with [TEST - would go to candidate]. Use this while setting up or testing replacement chains. Turn it off before your first real roster cycle. The System Status card shows an amber warning when test mode is on.

Quiet hours — SMS quiet hours start and end (Settings → Timing & behaviour) are set as times of day, e.g. 9:00 PM and 8:00 AM. Rostas will not send any SMS outside this window, including replacement chain advances. If a replacement request hits quiet hours, it waits until the quiet period ends before texting the next candidate. The default is 9pm–8am.

Replacement timers (Settings → Timing & behaviour) — three escalation speeds:

  • Normal (default 120 min) — used when the service is more than 24 hours away
  • Urgent (default 30 min) — used when the service is within 24 hours
  • Panic (default 10 min) — used when the service is within 2 hours

If volunteers are frequently timing out before responding, consider increasing the normal timer. If you're often scrambling at short notice, reducing the urgent or panic timers gets through the candidate list faster.

Message wording

Every SMS template has three protections built in to prevent accidental breakage.

  • Cost counter — SMS messages over 160 characters are split into two by carriers and charged as two texts. The counter below the textarea shows what the text will really cost, for example 122 / 160 · 1 credit. It turns amber when you're within 20 characters of the limit and red as soon as the text would cost more than one credit, and the Save button is disabled while it's red. The SMS templates are deliberately terse for this reason — when you edit one, the counter is protecting your church's SMS costs, not just formatting. Placeholders like [Name] count as typed; Preview SMS (below) shows the length once real names are filled in. Curly quotes (’ “ ”), long dashes (– —) and macrons (ā ē ī ō ū) are fine to type: Rostas swaps them for plain ones (' " - a e i o u) just before sending, and the counter already counts them as plain, so they cost nothing extra. Avoid emoji (smiley faces, praying hands and the like): Rostas can't swap them, and one emoji cuts the whole text's limit from 160 characters to 70. The counter shows this straight away (for example 62 / 70 · 1 credit (70 because of 🙏)), and if the text is then over 70 characters, Save is blocked with a message naming the emoji to remove.
  • Required placeholders — each template has a set of placeholders that must be present for it to work correctly. For example, the replacement request SMS must include [Link] — without it, the volunteer has nothing to tap. Missing placeholders are shown in red below the textarea. Save is blocked until all required placeholders are present.
  • Preview SMS — click this button to see the template rendered with realistic sample values substituted in ([Name] → Sarah, [Role] → Welcome Team, [Date] → a sample date, [Link] → a sample link). This shows you exactly what a volunteer will read, with the characters and credits for that filled-in text (links are counted at their real length). Longer names or roles than the samples can push a text near the limit into two credits, so leave some room. It does not send a real SMS.
ℹ️ All placeholder names use square brackets: [Name], [Role], [Date], [Link], [OrgName], [Deadline]. Never use curly braces — {name} will not be substituted.

Message wording lives in three categories: SMS (replacement request, manual replacement, taken off the roster, cross-roster ask, swap, availability nudge, reminder), App (the matching app notifications), and Email wording (roster email intro, arrival note and sign-off; reminder email opening, can't-make-it text and sign-off; swap email). The protections above apply to every text template.

Taken off the roster SMS (and Taken off the roster (app) under App) is the message a volunteer gets when you replace them on the Board, save them as declined with the tick box on, or take them off a roster with Let them know ticked — but only for dates whose roster had already gone out.

Troubleshooting

Start from what you've been told or what you can see. Each problem lists the checks in order, most likely first, so you can usually stop after the first one or two.

💡 Look at System Status first. Settings opens on it. If something is switched off, in test mode or out of credits, it says so there, with a button that fixes it. See Following a warning to its fix.

“A volunteer says they never got a text”

  1. Did they get an app notification instead? If the volunteer has the Rostas app with notifications on, Rostas sends an app notification and only texts if that doesn't arrive. Ask them to look in the app.
  2. Was it a day-before reminder? With the recommended reminder mode, Text non-responders, anyone who already confirmed the reminder email doesn't get the text. That's by design. Turn on Always text on their profile if they'd rather have one every time. See Reminder SMS mode.
  3. Was it during quiet hours? No texts go out overnight (9pm–8am unless you've changed it). A replacement request waits and goes in the morning.
  4. Is their SMS toggle on? Open their profile. If SMS is off, they (or someone) opted them out of texts. Turn it back on only if they ask you to.
  5. Is their mobile number right? Check it on their profile, digit by digit. It should be a full mobile number, such as +64 21 123 4567.
  6. Is SMS test mode on? While it is, every text goes to your phone instead, starting [TEST.
  7. Still a mystery? Look for that volunteer and date in the communication failures table in System Status. It says why a text didn't send.

“Nobody is getting texts”

  1. Are you out of credits? System Status says “You're out of SMS credits”. Buy a pack; texts start again straight away. See SMS Credits.
  2. Is SMS turned off, or SMS test mode on? Both show in System Status and in Quick Controls.
  3. Is it quiet hours? Texts resume when they end.
  4. Is your subscription paid? If a payment has failed, texts pause until it's sorted. Emails and app notifications keep working throughout.

“A volunteer replied to the text and nothing happened”

Rostas doesn't read replies to its texts, so a reply goes nowhere. Ask the volunteer to tap the link in the text instead: it opens a page where they answer Yes or No. If you already know their answer, record it yourself on the Board (Save as declined, or choose a replacement).

“A volunteer never got their roster email”

  1. Has the roster been sent? Volunteers hear nothing until you click Send notifications in Step 6 of the roster cycle. See Step 6 — Notify.
  2. Is it in their spam or junk folder? Ask them to look, and to mark it “not spam”.
  3. Is their email address right, and is their Email toggle on? Both are on their profile.
  4. Is Email test mode on? While it is, emails go to you instead.

“My link says the roster has changed” or “my link doesn't work”

  • “This roster has changed” or “someone else has already stepped in” is Rostas working properly: they tapped an older message after things moved on. Nothing was changed. See When an old link no longer applies.
  • The link stopped working altogether (an error or a sign-in page): if you recently issued them a new link, their old ones stop on purpose. Their next email or text carries the new one. See Issuing a new link. If it's happening to several volunteers, contact support.

“I was rostered on a date I said I couldn't do”

  1. Did they mark the date after the build? Availability given after a build doesn't move anyone already placed. Before you send, Rebuild roster picks it up. After you send, click their card and use Save as declined: Rostas then finds a replacement.
  2. Did they mark it Not sure rather than Can't make it? Then they're Pending (amber) and can say no from their link or the app.
  3. Check their availability on their profile to see what Rostas has on record. See Availability on the profile.

“I built a roster and almost every card is red and empty”

When most or all of a new roster is red, the usual reason is that no roles have been set for the volunteers yet. Rostas only places a volunteer in a role they've ticked, so a volunteer with no roles on this roster is never used, however available they are. This is common on a brand-new roster, especially straight after importing volunteers.

  1. Find who has no roles. On the Profiles page, pick the roster's pill and click No roles set. See Finding volunteers with no roles set.
  2. Set their roles. Either set them yourself, profile by profile (the arrows step only through the volunteers still left to do), or ask the volunteers to choose in Step 3 — Role Prefs of the roster cycle.
  3. Build again. Before you send, Rebuild roster on the Board places everyone afresh.

If only a few cards are red, see the next section instead.

“There are still red (unfilled) slots after the build”

  1. Have late availability replies come in? Click Refill roster on the Board. It fills only the empty slots and leaves everyone else where they are.
  2. Do enough people do that role? Only volunteers who ticked a role are rostered in it. If nearly every card is red, see Almost every card is red. Insights shows which roles are short.
  3. Are frequency caps holding people back? Someone who has reached their cap isn't used again until it allows. See Frequency cap.
  4. Fill the rest by hand: choose someone yourself, or ask someone from another roster.

“A card has said ‘In chain’ for ages”

  1. Is it quiet hours? The chain waits overnight and carries on in the morning.
  2. Is the roster paused, or SMS turned off? Check Builds → ⋯ and System Status.
  3. Has everyone been asked? When the list runs out, Rostas emails that roster's coordinator (or your Organisation email). Check your inbox.
  4. Don't wait: click the card and choose Pick replacement (see Choosing a replacement yourself). Once the slot is filled, the chain stops. If the original volunteer can come after all, choose Keep [name], stop requests.

“Why wasn't [name] asked to cover?”

Rostas only asks volunteers who have ticked that role on their profile, and leaves out anyone who marked themselves unavailable for that date. Frequency caps also apply. If you know they'd happily do it, tick the role on their profile, or choose them yourself on the Board.

“Sorry, unable to open the file at this time”

This Google message almost always means your browser is signed in to more than one Google account. Google can't tell which account should open Rostas, and you may see u/1/ in the address bar. Rostas itself is fine.

  • Every day: open Rostas from its icon in your Church profile (see Put Rostas on your desktop). That profile has only the church account in it, so the problem can't happen.
  • Right now, as a one-off: open the link in a private window (Chrome: Ctrl+Shift+N on Windows, ⌘+Shift+N on a Mac) and paste the address there.
  • Deleting u/1/ from the address doesn't help: Chrome puts it straight back. Signing in to the church account first doesn't help either.

When to contact tech support

Contact your tech support person if:

  • SMS messages are failing with error messages in the System Status communication failures table
  • Volunteer links have stopped working for several people (they open an error or a sign-in page)
  • System Status says SMS isn't switched on for your account
  • Something is producing repeated errors that don't resolve on their own
  • Anything requiring access to the Google Apps Script editor
📞 When contacting tech support, include a description of what you were doing, what you expected to happen, and what actually happened. A screenshot of any error message or the System Status panel is very helpful.

Glossary

The words Rostas uses on screen and in this guide, in plain English. Each one links to where it's explained in full.

BoardThe Roster Board: every date down the left, every role across the top, one card per slot. Where you check, swap and replace. See Reading the Board.
BuildRostas filling a stretch of dates with volunteers in one go, using availability, roles and fairness rules. A build covers only the dates in the This Build band; nothing is sent to volunteers until you click Send notifications. See Run a roster cycle.

RefillA button on the Board before you send: fills only the empty slots, using the latest availability. Everyone already placed stays put, including your swaps. Use it after late availability replies. See Checking the roster on the Board.

Connectors

Most organisations get their volunteers into Rostas by importing a CSV — it works for everyone and needs nothing more than a spreadsheet. But if your church or organisation already keeps its people in another system, or in a Google Sheet it keeps up to date, a connector can sync them into Rostas automatically, so you don't maintain two lists by hand.

ℹ️ Connectors sync identity only. A connector brings across each person's name, mobile, and email — nothing more. Everything about how they serve — which rosters they're in, the roles they do, their frequency, who they serve with — is set up and lives entirely inside Rostas. Your other system stays the source of truth for contact details; Rostas stays the source of truth for rostering.

Choosing your volunteer database

Everything to do with connectors is in one place: Settings → Controls → Volunteer database. It is the last row in the Quick controls list. Click it and the panel opens on the right.

At the top of the panel, Get volunteers from has four choices:

  • No database — you add volunteers by hand or from a CSV file. Nothing syncs at night. This is where every new Rostas starts.
  • Infoodle — see Infoodle below.
  • Planning Center — see Planning Center below.
  • Google Sheet — your own list of volunteers in a Google Sheet. See Google Sheet below.

Only one database is used at a time. The line under the choice shows what is in use now and when it last synced, for example In use now: Infoodle · last synced ….

Switching database

Pick a different database, set it up (the steps are under each one below), then press the button that appears under the panel — Use Infoodle for volunteers, Use Planning Center for volunteers, Use Google Sheet for volunteers, or Stop syncing volunteers. Rostas asks you to confirm in a yellow box inside the panel before it changes anything, and tells you if the database you are switching to isn't connected yet.

ℹ️ Switching never deletes volunteers. Everyone already in Rostas stays exactly as they are, with their roles, frequency and history. From the switch onwards, names and contact details are updated from the new database every night and whenever you press Sync now.

The sync status at the top of Settings

Across the top of Settings, a small Volunteers strip shows whether the connection is healthy and when it last synced, with a Sync now button to run a sync straight away. If no database is chosen it reads Not connected with a Set up button; if Planning Center needs attention it shows Connect or Reconnect; if Google Sheet is chosen but no sheet is saved yet it reads No Google Sheet saved with a Set up button. Each of those simply opens the Volunteer database panel.

Infoodle

Once Infoodle is connected, new and updated contacts flow in on their own — a volunteer added in Infoodle appears in your Profiles list without a re-import, and a changed phone number or email updates itself.

Where to find your Infoodle details

Rostas needs three things to connect: the address of your Infoodle site, a Client ID and a Client secret. You find all three in Infoodle itself — you don't need to contact Rostas or Infoodle support. In many churches the Rostas coordinator and the person who administers Infoodle are the same person; if that's not you, ask that person to follow these steps (or to sit with you while you do).

  • Infoodle address — the web address you use to log in to Infoodle, followed by /apiv2. For example, if you log in at https://yourchurch.infoodle.com, the address to enter is https://yourchurch.infoodle.com/apiv2.
  • Client ID — the username you normally log in to Infoodle with.
  • Client secret — a code Infoodle creates for your login. To make it:
    1. Log in to Infoodle with the login you want Rostas to use.
    2. On your Dashboard, choose My Account. (To do this for someone else's login, open that person's own profile, click the settings cog and choose Settings.)
    3. Scroll to API Credentials at the bottom of the page and generate your secret. Infoodle shows a warning first — read it, then continue.
    4. Copy the secret somewhere safe straight away. It gives access to your church's Infoodle data, so keep it private and don't email it around.
    5. When you're back on the My Account page, the Client secret box now holds the number. Select Authorise to finish.
💡 Good to know
  • There is one secret per Infoodle login, and Rostas can see only what that login is allowed to see in Infoodle. Use a login that can see the groups your volunteers are in.
  • Consider creating a separate Infoodle login just for Rostas, rather than using one person's own login. That way the connection doesn't break if that person leaves, changes role or changes their password.
  • If the connection ever stops working — for example after the secret is changed in Infoodle — enter the current details in Rostas and press Save and test connection again.

Connecting Infoodle

  1. Open Settings → Controls → Volunteer database and choose Infoodle.
  2. Fill in Infoodle address (it must start with https://), Client ID and Client secret, using the details from Where to find your Infoodle details above.
  3. Press Save and test connection. Rostas saves the details and signs in to Infoodle to check them. A green message confirms it worked; a red one says what went wrong.
  4. Press Use Infoodle for volunteers and confirm.

If the test fails, nothing is saved — the previous details are put back — so a typo can never break a sync that was already working. Once the secret is saved, the box shows Saved — leave blank to keep it; you only retype it to change it.

Which people are synced

Rostas syncs the people in your Infoodle groups with “Roster” in the name — for example “Sunday Roster” or “Music Roster”. To add someone to Rostas, add them to one of those groups in Infoodle. If you press Save and test connection and no group has “Roster” in its name yet, Rostas tells you to rename the groups your volunteers belong to, and then press Sync now.

If your church was set up with a fixed list of group numbers (under Advanced → Infoodle group IDs), Rostas keeps using exactly that list, and the panel says so. Clear that setting if you would rather use the “Roster” name rule.

When someone is removed in Infoodle, Rostas doesn't delete them out from under you. Instead it flags them with a "Deleted in Infoodle" marker in the Profiles list, so you can confirm they should go and remove them here deliberately — protecting you from losing a volunteer's history by accident.

Planning Center

Planning Center connects by signing in to your Planning Center account — there are no codes or keys to copy.

Connecting Planning Center

  1. Open Settings → Controls → Volunteer database and choose Planning Center. The panel shows Not connected yet.
  2. Press Connect Planning Center. The button changes to Open Planning Center sign-in — click it. Planning Center opens in a new tab.
  3. Sign in to Planning Center and approve the connection, then return to the Rostas tab. The panel says Waiting for you to sign in… and changes to Connected to your organisation's name as soon as you finish.
  4. Press Use Planning Center for volunteers and confirm.

Your church needs its Rostas licence key in place before the sign-in will work; if the panel says so, contact support@rostas.app.

Which people are synced

As with Infoodle, Rostas syncs the people in your Planning Center groups with “Roster” in the name (archived groups are ignored). Someone who leaves all of those groups is marked inactive in Rostas rather than deleted, so their history is kept.

Reconnecting or disconnecting

If Planning Center stops accepting the connection — for example the sign-in has expired or was revoked — the strip at the top of Settings turns red and reads Planning Center needs reconnecting. Press Reconnect and sign in again; volunteers aren't affected in the meantime, they just stop updating.

To stop using Planning Center, open the panel and press Disconnect. Rostas asks you to confirm, and reminds you that volunteers already in Rostas stay exactly as they are.

Google Sheet

If your church keeps its volunteer list in an ordinary spreadsheet, Rostas can follow it. Keep editing the sheet as you do now; every night, and whenever you press Sync now, Rostas brings across new people and changed names, mobiles and emails.

How your sheet should look

  • One person per row, with a heading row at the top. A title row above the headings is fine.
  • A column headed Name, or two columns headed First name and Surname. This is the only column you must have.
  • A column headed Email, and one headed Mobile or Phone. If you have both, Rostas uses Mobile. Common variations such as Cell, E-mail or Last name are recognised too.
  • Any other columns are ignored, so you can keep notes, addresses or anything else in the same sheet.
  • Type mobile numbers however you like (021 555 0001, +64 21 555 0001, (021) 555-0001); Rostas tidies them into one format.
ℹ️ Using Excel? Upload the file to Google Drive, open it, and choose File → Save as Google Sheets. Then keep your list in that Google Sheet. A file on your computer or in OneDrive can't be synced; import it instead.

Connecting your sheet

  1. Open Settings → Controls → Volunteer database and choose Google Sheet. The panel shows which Google account the sheet must be shared with.
  2. In your sheet, press Share, add that account and choose Viewer. If you made the sheet with that same account, it's already shared and you can skip this step.
  3. Copy the sheet's address from your browser's address bar and paste it into Link to the sheet. Rostas reads the tab that was open when you copied the link. To use a different tab, type its name in Tab name.
  4. Press Save and test. Rostas reads the sheet and tells you which columns it found, how many people are listed, and what the next sync would change, for example 5 added, 0 updated, 1 listed twice (skipped). Nothing changes in Rostas yet. If something is wrong, the message in red says what to fix.
  5. Press Use Google Sheet for volunteers and confirm, then press Sync now at the top of Settings. Your volunteers appear in Profiles.

What a sync does

  • New rows become new volunteers. Only names, mobiles and emails come across; roles, rosters and frequency are set in Rostas as usual.
  • Changed rows update the same volunteer. Rostas recognises people by their email, then their mobile, then their name, so you can fix a misspelt name or change a number without creating a second person.
  • A blank cell never wipes anything. If you clear someone's email in the sheet, Rostas keeps the one it has.
  • Someone listed twice (the same email or mobile on two rows) is added once. Rows with no name are skipped.
  • People already in Rostas from before (added by hand, imported, or from another database) who are also on the sheet with the same email or mobile are left exactly as they are, so nobody is doubled up.
  • Someone removed from the sheet is made inactive in Rostas, never deleted, so their history is kept. Add their row back and they become active again on the next sync.
⚠️ A safety catch for big changes. If the sheet is suddenly empty, or a sync would make more than half of the sheet's volunteers inactive (five or more people), Rostas changes nothing and the nightly sync stops there. This protects you from a sheet that was cleared or sorted by mistake. If the change is real, open the panel, press Save and test, and press Yes, make them inactive and sync.

To stop using the sheet, choose another database (or No database) in the panel. Volunteers already in Rostas stay exactly as they are.

Other connectors

Infoodle, Planning Center and Google Sheet are the connectors available today. Support for other church and volunteer platforms — such as Elvanto — is planned, so more organisations can sync rather than import over time.

ℹ️ Not on the list yet? CSV import works with every system in the meantime — most spreadsheets and databases can export a CSV. If a connector for your platform would help, let your Rostas contact know; it helps prioritise what's built next.
